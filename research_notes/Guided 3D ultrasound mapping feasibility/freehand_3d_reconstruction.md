# Freehand 3D Ultrasound Reconstruction and Probe-Pose Estimation: State of the Art (as of October 2026)

Scope note: These notes cover reconstruction of a 3D volume from a handheld 2D ultrasound (US) probe swept over a patient, with emphasis on how the per-frame probe pose is obtained (optical / electromagnetic (EM) trackers, IMU-only, camera-based inside-out and outside-in tracking, and fully image-only "sensorless/trackerless" deep learning), the accuracy achieved, the physics that makes the problem hard, datasets/challenges (TUS-REC 2024/2025), interactive coverage guidance, hardware requirements, and open-source toolkits. Seminal older work is included for context; 2020-2026 deep-learning work is prioritised. Every numeric claim carries an inline source; items I could not verify are listed under Gaps.

---

## Key Question 1: What drift / positional error do sensorless (image-only) methods achieve over a typical sweep, why does drift accumulate, and how do IMU-assisted methods compare with optical/EM tracking?

### Takeaway
Image-only deep-learning methods in 2024-2026 typically end a 100-400 mm forearm sweep with a global (accumulated) position error of roughly 5-12 mm (final drift rate ~5-15% of sweep length) on the best public benchmark, while frame-to-frame (local) errors stay at ~0.1 mm; drift accumulates because elevational (out-of-plane) motion is weakly observable from speckle and each small per-frame bias is integrated over hundreds of frames. Adding an IMU cuts final drift by roughly a third to a half (e.g., 18.3% -> 11.0% FDR on arm scans), probe-mounted or external camera tracking reaches ~1-4 mm, and commercial optical (0.12-0.25 mm RMS) and EM (0.5-1.4 mm RMS) trackers remain one to two orders of magnitude more accurate.

### Cited Findings

**Seminal / classical sensorless work**
- Prevost et al. (MICCAI 2017; Medical Image Analysis 2018, vol. 48, pp. 187-202, authors Prevost, Salehi, Jagoda, Kumar, Sprung, Ladikos, Bauer, Zettinig, Wein) introduced the first deep-learning model for sensorless freehand 3D US; it is consistently described as "the pioneering work" that later models build on — [arXiv 2502.03505](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03505v1); [DBLP listing confirming MedIA 48:187-202 (2018)](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/61/9219.html).
- Prevost's motivation: "Previously proposed approaches rely on physical models whose assumptions only hold on synthetic or phantom datasets, failing to translate to actual clinical acquisitions with sufficient accuracy" — [TUM CAMP copy of Prevost 2017 (cited via search snippet)](https://campar.cs.tum.edu/pub/prevost2017sensorless/prevost2017sensorless.pdf).
- Reported Prevost errors (as tabulated in the TUS-REC2024 challenge paper's literature table): MICCAI 2017 version — final drift (median) 18.30 mm on phantom (20 sweeps), 14.44 mm on forearms (88 sweeps, 12 volunteers), 40.56 mm on lower legs (12 sweeps), ground truth from a Stryker Navigation System III optical tracker. MedIA 2018 version added 600 forearm sweeps from 15 volunteers and 100 carotid sweeps from 10 volunteers; final drift (median) 18.30 mm phantom, 14.44 mm forearm, 19.69 mm calves, 10.42 mm on the larger forearm dataset — [TUS-REC2024 paper, Appendix Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Prevost et al. "first demonstrated the promise of boosting reconstruction performance by simply concatenating the IMU orientation with the fully connected layer of neural networks, due to the low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the IMU acceleration" — [Luo et al., MoNet, arXiv 2207.00177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00177).
- Pre-deep-learning sensorless approaches used speckle decorrelation, which "uses speckle correlation between adjacent images to estimate relative motion and decomposes it into in-plane and out-of-plane parts" — [MoNetV2, arXiv 2506.15835](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1).

**DCL-Net / DC2-Net (Guo et al., RPI)**
- DCL-Net (MICCAI 2020) "utilizes 3D convolutions over a US video segment for feature extraction. An embedded self-attention module makes the network focus on the speckle-rich areas for better spatial movement prediction"; a case-wise correlation loss stabilises training; targeted at transrectal prostate US — [arXiv 2006.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07694).
- DCL-Net reported values: prostate TRUS (640 videos, 640 patients, EM tracker ground truth) distance error 10.33 mm, final drift 17.39 mm — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- DC2-Net (Guo et al., IEEE TBME 2023): Dataset A (TRUS, 618 patients) distance error 5.52 +/- 2.86 mm, final drift 10.20 +/- 8.47 mm, drift rate 9.64 +/- 8.14%; Dataset B (transabdominal/transperineal, 100 scans) 5.87 +/- 2.68 mm, 9.85 +/- 5.74 mm, drift rate 14.58 +/- 12.76% — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2); DC2-Net also reported prostate Dice 0.89 — [search summary of TBME paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00274).
- When used as a baseline by a rival group, DCL-Net had final drift rate (FDR) 12.78% (SD 14.87) on DDH sequences and 12.47% (SD 8.49) on fetal sequences — [Luo et al., Self Context and Shape Prior, arXiv 2108.00274](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00274).
- Definition used across this literature: "final drift ... is the drift of the final frame of a sequence, measured as the distance between the frame centers according to the real relative position and the estimated relative position" — [arXiv 2108.00274](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00274).

**UCL/KCL (Li, Hu, Vercauteren, Barratt, Clarkson)**
- Long-term dependency model (Li et al., IEEE TBME 2024, vol. 71(3), pp. 1033-1042): "Extending the dependency to about 400 frames at 20 fps reduced accumulated error by as much as 82.4% relative to the baseline"; the gain depended on sequence length, transformation interval and scanning protocol, and "did not depend on the use of recurrent networks with long-short term modules"; "Greater performance was gained from representative protocol patterns, than from representative anatomical features" — [arXiv 2310.10248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10248); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10179008).
- Earlier ISBI 2023 finding: the best model predicted transformations between moderately spaced frames (interval < 10 frames at 20 fps) and "little benefit was observed by adding frames more than one second away" — [search summary citing ISBI 2023 paper](https://deepai.org/publication/trackerless-freehand-ultrasound-with-sequence-modelling-and-auxiliary-transformation-over-past-and-future-frames).
- Reported values from this group on 228 forearm scans / 19 volunteers (NDI Polaris Vicra GT): final drift 36.82 +/- 28.01 mm (CNN) and 36.48 +/- 20.77 mm (LSTM) without consistency loss; with multi-transformation prediction at M=100: 6.56 +/- 7.53 mm, M=140: 6.53 +/- 5.98 mm; another variant at M=100: 7.24 +/- 8.33 mm — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Nonrigid reconstruction (Li et al., MICCAI 2024): on 720 scans / 60 subjects / >357,000 frames, co-optimising rigid transforms with a nonrigid deformation field reduced global pixel reconstruction error from 18.48 mm to 16.51 mm (~10.7%) versus rigid-only baselines; motivation is "observed nonrigid deformation due to soft tissue motion during scanning"; code and data released — [arXiv 2407.05767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05767); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10194309).

**Shenzhen University MUSIC Lab (Luo, Yang, Ni) — IMU-assisted**
- MoNet (MICCAI 2022): first to use IMU acceleration to estimate elevational displacement; uses a temporal, multi-branch structure to mine "low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) acceleration" and an online self-supervised scheme using IMU data as weak labels — [arXiv 2207.00177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00177). MoNet values: arm FDR 12.75 +/- 9.05%, ADR 19.05 +/- 11.46%; carotid FDR 15.67 +/- 8.37%, ADR 25.08 +/- 9.34% (EM ground truth) — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- OSCNet / Multi-IMU (MICCAI 2023, arXiv 2306.16197): multiple IMUs with modal-level and sequence-level self-consistency; benchmark-aggregator figures FDR 13.06% (arm) and 14.17% (carotid) — [arXiv 2306.16197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16197v3).
- MoNetV2 (2025): WT901C-232 IMU bound to a Mindray L12-4s linear probe on a Mindray M8, 30 fps, 248x260 px at 0.15 mm, EM (trakSTAR) ground truth; 50 volunteers; datasets: Arm 583 scans (avg 386.39 mm; linear, curved, loop, sector), Carotid 432 scans (avg 241.25 mm), Thyroid 290 scans (avg 66.25 mm). Results (FDR / ADR / MD mm / HD mm / MEA deg): Arm MoNetV2 11.04% / 18.26% / 28.84 / 26.24 / 3.00 vs image-only ResNet18 21.13% / 31.29% / 55.01 / 51.10 / 7.36 and DC2-Net 18.12% / 26.63% / 48.26 / 48.15 / 7.02; Carotid MoNetV2 12.11% / 23.78% / 20.75 mm vs ResNet18 21.47% / 32.56% / 37.53 mm; Thyroid MoNetV2 17.88% / 26.34% / 8.93 mm vs ResNet18 26.95% / 43.88% / 13.04 mm. Ablation: adding the IMU alone reduced arm FDR from 18.30% to 16.27%; full model 11.04% — [arXiv 2506.15835](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1).
- MoNetV2's stated reasons for drift: image-only estimation "is limited by the low SNR and contrast of ultrasound, especially along the elevation direction"; per-frame errors "accumulate over the sequence, which lead to drift phenomena, causing distortions or displacements in reconstruction"; varying scanning velocities/tactics shift inter-frame content distribution; "tissue compression and jitter, particularly in thyroid scans, add further error" — [arXiv 2506.15835](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1).
- Earlier Luo results (simulation from 3D volumes): DDH FDR 5.44 +/- 3.03%, fetus FDR 9.94 +/- 4.41% (ConvLSTM+SSL+ADL); later 3D DDH 10.68 +/- 11.21%, fetal 13.31 +/- 10.94%, 2D spine (EM) 10.82 +/- 5.36%; RecON online: arm FDR 10.01 +/- 5.7%, carotid 10.90 +/- 5.3% — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- RecON (Luo et al. 2023) FDR 25.90% / ADR 30.36% and MoNet FDR 12.75% / ADR 19.05% on ~80 mm linear sweeps, as tabulated by a 2026 camera-tracking paper; Dai et al. (2024) FDR 2.74%, ADR 3.35%, MD 2.52 mm — [MLRecon, arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).

**2025-2026 sensorless state of the art**
- DualTrack (Wilson, Ronchetti, Gobl, Markova, Rosenzweig, Prevost, Mousavi, Zettinig; ImFusion + Queen's; ASMUS@MICCAI 2025; winner of TUS-REC2025): dual encoder — a 3D ResNet18 local encoder on full-resolution frames over small temporal windows (speckle cues) plus a 2D ResNet18-per-frame global encoder at 224x224 with a temporal transformer (anatomical context), fused by a cross-attention transformer decoder; global input subsampled every ~8 frames. On 1,248 TUS-REC forearm scans / 53 volunteers (1,176 train / 72 test): GPE 4.93 mm, FDR 5.10%, max drift 8.33 mm, vs DCL-Net 10.77 mm / 11.68% / 18.75 mm, 2-frame CNN 8.90 mm / 8.93%, MoNet 9.19 mm / 8.87%, Hybrid Transformer 6.01 mm / 6.22% / 10.00 mm (Wilcoxon p <= 0.005). Ablation: local-only GPE 7.36 mm, global-only 11.26 mm, coupled single encoder 5.92 mm. Tested USFM, MedSAM and iBOT foundation backbones for the global branch; the plain 2D CNN performed best, iBOT second. Runtime ~0.73 s per 546-frame sweep on an RTX Quadro 6000, <7 GB. Worst test case still ~8 mm GPE with an angular offset at the end; "The local-only model failed to resolve out-of-plane motion and lost track after the first turn" — [arXiv 2509.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09530v1); ImFusion states DualTrack "won the TUS-REC 2025 challenge" — [imfusion.com](https://www.imfusion.com/).
- UltrasODM (PMLR v317, 2026; Anand et al.): dual-stream optical-flow + Dual-Mamba temporal network with Bayesian per-frame uncertainty and clinician alerts; relative to UltrasOM it reduced drift by 15.2%, distance error 12.1%, Hausdorff 10.1% (percentages only); code at github.com/AnandMayank/UltrasODM — [PMLR v317](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v317/anand26a.html).
- "Seeing Globally, Refining Locally" (arXiv 2607.12398, July 2026): dual external cameras for globally stable localisation plus B-mode for local refinement; US-only pose estimation gives ">13 mm drift" whereas US + dual-cam gives 1.67 mm (FUSION-J) and 1.29 mm (FUSION-L) average trajectory drift; in vivo forearm arteries Hausdorff 1.58 mm; in the US-only branch the method reduced ADR to 5.80% and AD to 15.14 mm; evaluated on TUS-REC2025 — [arXiv 2607.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12398); [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12398).
- Other tabulated 2021-2024 sensorless results (forearm unless stated, optical GT): Xie et al. distance error 4.73 mm / final drift 8.55 mm (100 sweeps); Ning et al. forearm final drift 15.84 mm, clinical 35.17 mm; Leblanc et al. femoral artery (111 sweeps, 18 volunteers) frame-to-frame error 0.28 mm, final drift 17.22 mm, Dice 0.90; Miura et al. forearm final drift 75.8 mm (and 96.46 mm across three datasets in a later paper); Dou et al. prostate 3D TRUS (1,928 scans) distance error 4.38 mm / final drift 7.06 mm; El Hadramy et al. ex vivo swine liver (trakSTAR) FDR 23.11 +/- 11.6%, ADR 28.71 +/- 12.97% — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).

**TUS-REC benchmark error levels (see Q3 for full tables)**
- TUS-REC2024 (forearm, ~500 frames/scan, 20 fps, optical GT): best global point error (GPE) 5.97-7.19 mm with local point error (LPE) ~0.10 mm; baseline 12.49 mm / 0.135 mm — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- TUS-REC2025 (adds rotation/tilt): best GPE 9.25 mm (ImFusion), baseline 26.11 mm; local errors 0.12-0.26 mm — [TUS-REC2025 leaderboard](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/leaderboard.html).
- The challenge found "Local errors (LPE, LLE) stay comparatively bounded, while global errors (GPE, GLE) grow with scan length"; "longer sequences may lead to greater drift"; correlation of error with scan length r ~0.3-0.5 per scan and r ~0.58-0.78 at subject level; S-shaped scans and parallel orientation increase errors — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).

**IMU-only and visual-inertial tracking**
- IMU-only probe tracking: in a 2024 patellar-tracking study the authors "concluded that IMU-only tracking is not yet practical because its drift rate is high" and that adding more data did not meaningfully improve deep inertial odometry; visual-inertial odometry (VIO) "clearly beat IMU-only tracking on both absolute and relative pose error, and came close to an optical tracking system"; adding a start/end loop-closure prior reduced IMU-only RPE/APE but "it remains higher than VIO"; residual drift was corrected with ICP constrained to position and yaw — [arXiv 2404.15847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15847); [review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/3d-freehand-ultrasound-using-visual-inertial-and-deep-inertial-odometry-for-measuring-patellar-tracking).
- Same study: average bone reconstruction error 1.25 mm (VIO) vs 1.21 mm (motion capture) with a wireless handheld scanner — [arXiv 2404.15847 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15847).
- Mikaeili et al.: Bosch BNO055 IMU as "ground truth" on a phantom, 3 sweeps / 2,520 frames, MAE ~0.4-0.8 deg rotation, ~1.5-2.4 mm translation — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- A probe-mounted binocular camera + IMU fused by an unscented Kalman filter on Lie groups (He et al., Ultrasound in Medicine & Biology 2024, 50(8)) reported accumulated position error 3.78 mm and orientation error 0.36 deg — [bohrium summary](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/19782632); [MLRecon related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- An "IMU-weighted method had reported translational and rotational errors of 10.24 mm and 1.55 deg, respectively, which the authors said remained inadequate for clinical use" — [search summary citing arXiv 2510.15668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15668v1).

**Camera-based tracking (inside-out and outside-in)**
- Busam et al. 2018 (markerless inside-out): miniature mono/stereo/active-depth rig on the probe, relocalised in an adaptive map of the OR via visual SLAM; "Feature-based binocular SLAM is identified as the most promising method"; compared with a commercial optical tracker and a robot for transrectal prostate compounding; the inside-out approach "even outperforms the outside-in system in terms of rotational accuracy"; ArUco markers "viable only for approximate positioning within a room" — [arXiv 1804.01708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01708); [search summary](https://cris.fau.de/publications/290930014). A later paper tabulates this ORB-SLAM approach at APE 2.65 mm (0.74) / ARE 1.99 deg — [MLRecon](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- Buchanan et al. 2024 (IEEE MeMeA): probe-mounted stereo camera VIO, errors "comparable to motion capture", APE ~4.00 mm — [MLRecon](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- Zhang et al. 2025 (IEEE/ASME TMech) and arXiv 2510.15668 (Zhang, Huang, Meng, Navab, Jiang): probe-mounted monocular cameras observing a textured planar workspace with sim-in-the-loop visual servoing; APE 1.46 mm (back-and-forth), 1.98 mm (spiral); Hausdorff to reference reconstruction 0.359 mm (vascular phantom), 1.171 mm (printed cone), 0.858 mm (human arm) — [arXiv 2510.15668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15668); [MLRecon](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- MLRecon (arXiv 2603.00990, March 2026; Zhang, Tu, Wang, Yan, Ying, Chen): single external Orbbec Astra 2 RGB-D camera, markerless; SAM 2-based (SANSA) mask initialisation + FoundationPose 6D tracking with a pre-scanned probe CAD model, divergence detection and re-initialisation, two-stage temporal-conv refinement; N-wire calibration; tracking up to 140 Hz on an RTX 3080 Ti laptop, US-pose pairs at 20 Hz. Results: linear FDR 0.36% / ADR 0.27% / MD 1.85 mm / MRE 1.12 deg over ~250 mm; back-and-forth APE 0.88 mm (0.45) / ARE 0.48 deg over ~560 mm; spiral APE 1.44 mm / ARE 0.92 deg over ~480 mm — [arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- Low-cost ArUco dodecahedron on probe + Intel RealSense D435i RGB at 60 Hz (De Sanctis et al., Diagnostics 2024): mean position error norm 2.78-2.86 mm (SD norm 5.5-6.3 mm) vs a 10-camera Qualisys reference; error grows with distance from camera; no lens calibration; reconstruction out of scope — [PMC11275361](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11275361/).
- RGB-D camera + 3D-printed marker AR framework: target pose localisation error 5.6-5.9 mm and -3.9 to 4.2 deg — [deepai summary of arXiv 2205.04350](https://deepai.org/publication/a-novel-augmented-reality-ultrasound-framework-using-an-rgb-d-camera-and-a-3d-printed-marker).
- UltrARsound (HoloLens 2 depth camera tracking retroreflective spheres on the probe): median 1.98 mm / 1.81 deg static with Kalman filter, 2.81 mm / 1.70 deg dynamic — [PMC9515035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9515035/).
- Visual-inertial (Intel RealSense T265) 3D US + photoacoustic: pose jitter SD ~0.13-0.2 mm per axis; volumes not significantly different from ground truth — [PMC10278605](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10278605/).

**Optical and EM tracker accuracy (reference tiers)**
- NDI Polaris Vicra (used as TUS-REC GT): stated 3D RMS volumetric accuracy <= 0.25 mm, repeatability <= 0.20 mm — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2). NDI Polaris Vega ST/VT/XT 0.12-0.15 mm RMS, Lyra 0.20-0.25 mm — [NDI Vega comparison](https://www.ndigital.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/10006213-Vega-Model-Comparison-Email.pdf).
- NDI Aurora EM: ~0.48 mm RMS (cube volume, 6-DOF sensor), ~0.70 mm RMS (dome); 3D Guidance (trakSTAR family) mid-range transmitter 1.40 mm RMS — [US patent 11596367 quoting manufacturer specs](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11596367); [ndigital.com](https://www.ndigital.com/?p=163).
- Head-to-head: lateral skull base, optical median 0.22 mm vs EM 0.99 mm (p < 0.001) — [search summary](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100024532265_0x000001); laparoscopy study: at 50 mm separation optical 0.210/0.233 mm vs EM 0.214 mm RMS, at 250 mm optical 1.031/1.178 mm vs EM 0.367 mm; EM-tracked laparoscopic US probe point-localisation RMS 1.3 mm — [PMC6282846](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6282846/).
- EM metal distortion: a conductive disk raised max EM error from 1.5 mm to 10.9 mm — [search summary](https://springerplus.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/2193-1801-2-90/tables/1).
- End-to-end clinical freehand 3D US with optical tracking: volume/length discrepancy ~3% (3 ml, 1 mm) (Cenni et al. 2016) — [TAU CRIS](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/the-reliability-and-validity-of-a-clinical-3d-freehand-ultrasound/); gastrocnemius vs MRI: volume +1.1 +/- 3.8%, length -3.0 +/- 5.4 mm — [PubMed 19375081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19375081/).

### Inferences
- A practical accuracy ladder (same-order estimates drawn from the figures above): optical 0.1-0.3 mm RMS; EM 0.5-1.5 mm RMS (worse near metal); external RGB-D + foundation-model tracking ~1-3 mm; probe-mounted camera/VIO 1.5-4 mm; camera+US fusion ~1.3-1.7 mm drift; image+IMU sensorless ~11-18% FDR (tens of mm on 250-400 mm sweeps); image-only sensorless 5-12 mm GPE / 5-15% FDR on benchmark forearm sweeps, 20-30% FDR on harder anatomy (liver, long arm sweeps); IMU-only not viable beyond a few seconds.
- "Local" (frame-to-frame) accuracy is essentially solved (~0.1 mm on TUS-REC), so remaining error is almost entirely low-frequency bias integrated over the sweep; this is why the 2025 winners combine a local speckle branch with a global anatomy-context branch, and why camera fusion or loop closure yields step changes.
- Reported FDR values are not comparable across papers: sweep length, anatomy, frame rate, probe type, simulated vs real scans, and the GT tracker all differ (e.g., DDH results are from simulated slicing of 3D volumes).

- I could not retrieve the Prevost 2018 MedIA abstract directly (ScienceDirect/PubMed blocked); the numbers above come from the TUS-REC2024 literature table. The exact % improvement attributable to the IMU in that paper is not confirmed.

## Key Question 2: What physical properties of ultrasound make 3D reconstruction from freehand sweeps hard?

### Takeaway
The dominant obstacle is that motion perpendicular to the image plane (elevational) is only weakly encoded, via speckle decorrelation whose sign is ambiguous and whose calibration assumes fully developed speckle that real tissue rarely shows; on top of that, probe pressure deforms soft tissue nonrigidly, appearance is strongly view-dependent (shadowing, reflection), and low SNR/contrast limits per-frame motion estimates, so errors integrate into drift.

### Cited Findings
- Elevational direction is the bottleneck: "improving reconstruction accuracy is still hampered by difficult elevational displacement estimation and large cumulative drift" — [MoNet, arXiv 2207.00177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00177); "the low signal-to-noise ratio of ultrasound images limits how precisely motion can be estimated from images alone, which is particularly evident in the elevation direction, which is perpendicular to the image plane" — [MoNetV2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1).
- Speckle decorrelation physics: imperfect elevational focusing means neighbouring frames share scatterers within overlapping resolution cells, so patch correlation falls with elevational separation; calibration maps the correlation coefficient onto the beam pattern — [US patent 5655535 / 5899861 summaries](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5655535).
- Sign ambiguity: decorrelation "approaches may have difficulties to distinguish a forward motion from a backward motion" — [US patent 12039741](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12039741); Laporte (McGill PhD 2009) combined "combinatorial optimisation techniques and robust statistics ... to recover non-monotonic motion and frame intersections" — [McGill eScholarship](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/kp78gg87z).
- Fully developed speckle (FDS) assumption fails in vivo: the method "requires the B-scans to contain mostly FDS pattern but this kind of pattern is rare in scans of real tissue" — [Carleton thesis](https://curve.carleton.ca/15940bd5-fc2f-46a3-b9b2-4fd9dd6aa909); performance "limited due to several factors, including the inherent variance of pure speckle patterns" (Lee et al., SPIE 2021, who used a gel pad and SVD-improved decorrelation curves) — [SPIE 11792](https://astronomicaltelescopes.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11792/117920U/Elevational-motion-estimation-for-3D-ultrasound-with-machine-learning-and/10.1117/12.2590727.full).
- In-plane vs out-of-plane: "algorithms like 'optical flow' allow estimating the in-plane motion quite reliably, while out-of-plane motion remains harder" — [US patent 11534138](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11534138).
- Probe pressure deformation: "varying pressure across a scan means different parts of the anatomy are captured at different degrees of compression, producing artefact-ridden reconstructions"; Treece et al. corrected this with "a rigid in-plane translation and a non-rigid depth compression to each B-scan to maximize correlation with the successive image", later chaining corrections with position-sensor data "to limit error accumulation"; RF-based elastography-style correction was "more precise in certain circumstances" — [Cambridge Treece group](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/Main/GMT_3dus); [treece_tr491](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/biomed/treece_tr491.html); [liner review](https://liner.com/review/correction-probe-pressure-artifacts-in-freehand-3d-ultrasound).
- Dahmani et al. (SPIE 2017) used a 2D biomechanical model whose tissue parameters are co-estimated with the deformation field by maximising mutual information to a deformation-free reference — [SPIE 10133](https://remotesensing.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10133/101331D/Model-based-correction-of-ultrasound-image-deformations-due-to-probe/10.1117/12.2254636.full).
- Multi-sweep misalignment from pressure: "due to the inherent local and non-uniform compression of the underlying anatomy (caused by the ultrasound probe) the adjacent sweeps often exhibit poor alignment and discontinuities" (Schulte zu Berge, Salehi, Wein et al., VCBM 2018) — [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/vcbm20181223).
- Nonrigid motion is now modelled in trackerless DL: "probe pressure and patient movement cause soft-tissue undergoing nonrigid deformation" (Li et al. MICCAI 2024) — [arXiv 2407.05767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05767).
- View dependence: earlier INR models "ignore view-dependent changes in appearance and geometry that are intrinsic to ultrasound imaging" (Ultra-NeRF) — [PMLR v227](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v227/wysocki24a.html); UlRe-NeRF adds a "directional MLP for view-dependent reflection intensity" because naive INRs produce "defects that violate physical laws" — [arXiv 2408.00860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00860).
- Gaussian splatting must be adapted: "direct application of 3DGS to ultrasound is non-trivial due to fundamental physical differences" (UltraGS) — [arXiv 2511.07743](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07743v2); UltraGauss "models probe-plane intersections in 3D, which aligns with how acoustic images are formed" — [arXiv 2505.05643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05643).
- Protocol/velocity sensitivity: TUS-REC2024 found S-shape scans give "the largest errors and variability", parallel orientation and proximal-to-distal direction increase errors, and generalisation across "scan length and scanning protocol" is unsolved — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2); Li et al. found protocol diversity mattered more than anatomical diversity in training — [arXiv 2310.10248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10248).
- Dynamic anatomy: for moving fetuses "external trackers fail", motivating image-based SLAM (EchoFusion) — [arXiv 1807.10583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10583).
- Tracker-side limits: optical needs line of sight, EM is distorted by metal (max error 1.5 -> 10.9 mm with a conductive disk) — [PMC3955490 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3955490/table/Tab1); [Queen's EM/optical fusion paper](https://labs.cs.queensu.ca/perklab/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/02/Lang2009.pdf).

### Inferences
- Because the elevational observability comes from speckle statistics, any image processing that destroys speckle (speckle reduction, heavy compression, low frame rate) directly degrades sensorless tracking; TUS-REC explicitly acquired with "no speckle reduction".
- Deformation and out-of-plane ambiguity interact: a nonrigid compression can mimic or mask elevational motion, so image-only methods are systematically biased on compressible tissue (thyroid, abdomen) relative to bony forearm.

### Gaps
- I did not find a quantitative, modern (2020+) study isolating shadowing/acoustic-shadow regions as a measured cause of sensorless pose error; the effect is asserted qualitatively only.
- No paper retrieved gives a direct measurement of how much speckle-reduction filters (common on clinical scanners) degrade sensorless accuracy.

## Key Question 3: What open datasets and challenges exist, and what were the winning results?

### Takeaway
TUS-REC (UCL/KCL, MICCAI 2024 and 2025) is the only public benchmark with optically tracked in vivo sweeps at scale (2,040 forearm scans / ~1.0 M frames in 2024, plus 170 rotation/tilt scans in 2025); the 2024 winner (FiMoNet, Shenzhen MUSIC Lab) reached ~7 mm global / 0.10 mm local error, the 2025 winner (ImFusion, DualTrack) 9.25 mm global on the harder rotational protocol, and the organisers conclude the task "is not yet solved to a clinically satisfactory degree."

### Cited Findings

**TUS-REC2024**
- Organisers: Qi Li, Yuliang Huang, Shaheer U. Saeed, Dean C. Barratt, Matthew J. Clarkson (UCL), Tom Vercauteren (KCL), Yipeng Hu (UCL) — [TUS-REC site](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/).
- Data: 85 volunteers, both forearms, 2,040 scans, 1,025,448 frames; Ultrasonix (BK) scanner, curvilinear 4DC7-3/40 probe, NDI Polaris Vicra via PLUS Toolkit, 20 fps, 480x640 px (~0.2 mm/px), depth 9 cm, 6 MHz, no speckle reduction; split 50 train / 3 val / 32 test subjects; test set 768 scans / 384,105 frames (~500 frames per scan); protocols: straight line, C and S shapes; parallel and perpendicular to the arm; distal-to-proximal and proximal-to-distal; 24 scans per subject in train — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2); [Zenodo train part 2](https://zenodo.org/records/11180795).
- Participation: 43 registered teams, 6 teams / 25 participants submitted 21 valid dockerized solutions; approaches: state space model, recurrent model, registration-driven volume refinement, attention, physics-informed — [arXiv 2506.21765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21765v2).
- Leaderboard (normalised final score FS; raw GPE / GLE / LPE / LLE in mm; runtime s): 1. FiMoNet, MUSIC Lab (Shenzhen Univ. + RayShape) FS 0.852, 7.191 / 6.281 / 0.097 / 0.084, 9.2 s; 2. - Findings: "no team consistently outperform others on all metrics"; FlowNet had lowest global error but weakest local score; ensembling and pretraining helped; conclusion: "not yet solved to a clinically satisfactory degree"; limitations: forearm-only, need unseen test data — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Training/validation data public under citation requirement — [TUS-REC2024 site](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/TUS-REC2024).

**TUS-REC2025**
- New protocol "with more diverse probe movement such as rotating and tilting at various angles" in addition to 2024 protocols; 85 volunteers, 170 scans, ~260k frames, optical GT, CC BY 4.0 — [Zenodo 15119085](https://zenodo.org/records/15119085); timeline: submissions 7 Jul - 31 Aug 2025, winners 1 Sep 2025, MICCAI event 27 Sep 2025, post-challenge deadline 13 Oct 2025; baseline code github.com/QiLi111/TUS-REC2025-Challenge_baseline — [TUS-REC2025 site](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/).

MUSIC Lab (Shenzhen) 0.620, 9.851 / 8.116 / 0.138 / 0.116, 107.5 s; 3. COCHE (HK) 0.503, 13.892 / 10.705 / 0.172 / 0.143; 4. AGH-MedApp (Krakow) 0.383, 18.605 / 15.955; 5 (post-challenge) T3DR (AGH) 0.310, 15.956 / 13.623; 6. AMI-Lab (Pusan) 0.301, 21.800 / 19.645, 10.4 s; 7. SMART Lab (Shanghai Univ.) 0.235, 23.420 / 19.527; 8. QBME (SJTU) 0.188, 25.708 / 21.728; Baseline (UCL) 0.163, 26.110 / 23.681 / 0.214 / 0.181 — [TUS-REC2025 leaderboard](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/leaderboard.html).
- The ImFusion entry is DualTrack (see Q1) — [imfusion.com](https://www.imfusion.com/); [arXiv 2509.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09530v1).

**Other datasets used in the literature (mostly not public)**
- Prevost: Stryker-tracked phantom/forearm/calf/carotid sweeps (up to 600 forearm sweeps, 15 volunteers) — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).

- Fetal brain: INTERGROWTH-21st 3D volumes used for slice-to-volume pose work (Yeung et al.) — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).

### Inferences
- Across the two challenge years, global error roughly doubled for the same groups when rotation/tilt was added (FiMoNet 7.2 mm in 2024 vs MUSIC Lab 9.9 mm in 2025; baseline 12.5 -> 26.1 mm), indicating that rotational/tilting protocols are the current frontier.
- Runtime is not the bottleneck: all top entries process a ~500-frame sweep in 9-110 s offline, and DualTrack reports <1 s, so near-real-time sensorless reconstruction is feasible.

### Gaps
- The TUS-REC2025 challenge summary paper (with method descriptions for each 2025 team) was not found; only the leaderboard and DualTrack paper are available.
- Per-team method descriptions for 2024 (FiMoNet, RecuVol, FlowNet) are in the challenge paper but were not extracted in detail here.

## Key Question 4: Has anyone built an interactive system that guides the operator to "fill in" missing parts of a 3D volume (coverage maps, next-best-view for ultrasound)?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only at prototype level: the most explicit is Qian Lin's 2026 MIT thesis (advisor Brian Anthony) that plans probe motion primitives to maximise incremental voxel coverage with a 95% coverage stopping rule and live movement cues, validated on phantoms/simulation; older building blocks are the SlicerIGT/PLUS "scout scan then live high-resolution reconstruction until sufficient image density" workflow, a 2018 real-time freehand volumetric display, patents on cumulative-coverage displays, and 2026 trackerless work (UltrasODM) that alerts the operator to re-scan high-uncertainty regions. No clinically validated "next-best-view" ultrasound coverage guidance system was found.

### Cited Findings
- MIT thesis "Integrated Path Planning System for Freehand Ultrasound Scanning" (Qian Lin, MIT MechE, issued Feb 2026, advisor Brian W. Anthony): "explicitly optimizes incremental voxel coverage of the reconstructed volume, incorporating an occlusion-aware coverage objective to reduce under-sampled regions"; guidance from "a constrained set of probe motion primitives consisting of translation and rotation"; "Scan completion is determined automatically using a coverage saturation criterion (e.g., 95% target voxel coverage)"; real-time interface "provides instantaneous probe-movement cues and live image feedback"; metrics: voxel coverage recall, Dice, coverage efficiency, "overconfidence under localization noise"; evaluation on phantoms and CT-label-based simulation with pose-uncertainty stress testing; explicitly excludes contact force, tissue deformation, speed-of-sound correction — [MIT DSpace 1721.1/169967](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/169967).
- Related MIT talk: ongoing work on "a HoloLens 2 app designed to efficiently guide a user through collecting a complete ultrasound scan over a given volume" — [MIT.nano event](https://mitnano.mit.edu/node/3400).
- SlicerIGT/PLUS two-stage workflow: "quick low-resolution scouting followed by high-resolution live volume reconstruction ... within a region of interest defined in the scout scan, live volume reconstruction can be performed by continuous scanning until sufficient image density is achieved" — [PMC4437512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4437512).
- Real-time volumetric freehand scanning (SPIE 2018): "This visual feedback can guide the sonographer during freehand scanning to improve the quality of the reconstructed 3DUS images" — [SPIE 10580](https://medicalimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10580/105800E/Real-time-volumetric-ultrasound-imaging-using-free-hand-scanning/10.1117/12.2293817.full).
- Patent on scan completeness: real-time display of "the cumulative area of the transducer positions where the ultrasound images were generated" to show exam completeness and "guide the ultrasound user to scan the regions not yet covered" (breast) — [US 11839511](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11839511).
- Older patent: display "a 3D guide image or map of the source volume and during the imaging process displaying the current scan plane on the 3D map" for novices — [US 7925068](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7925068); Philips patent on feedback toward a target view by matching live images to an atlas/CT/MR — [US 10702248](https://patents.justia.com/patent/10702248); Caption Health "volume-based ultrasound guidance" — [US 12,484,880](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week48/OG/html/1541-1/US12484880-20251202.html).
- Trackerless uncertainty-driven re-scan prompts: UltrasODM "issues alerts suggesting that the operator re-scan highlighted regions or slow the sweep" when Bayesian per-frame uncertainty exceeds clinician-calibrated thresholds — [PMLR v317](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v317/anand26a.html).
- Mixed-reality overlays: HoloPOCUS (ASMUS@MICCAI 2023) overlays 3D reconstructions in the user's view and reports "significant improvements in navigation duration" in a phantom task — [arXiv 2308.13823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13823); a JHU wireless AR US system using SLAM reports 0.983 mm reprojection error — [JHU PULSE Lab IUS 2025](https://pulselab.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Zhang_IEEE_IUS_2025.pdf); UltrARsound HoloLens 2 tracking accuracy 1.98 mm / 1.81 deg static — [PMC9515035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9515035/).
- Sensorless 4D fetal: EchoFusion combines a Residual 3D U-Net for "tracking semantics" with SLAM to track and compound moving fetuses without external trackers — [arXiv 1807.10583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10583).

### Inferences
- The components for a coverage-guided system exist separately (real-time compounding in PLUS, coverage/occupancy criteria in Lin's thesis, uncertainty flags in UltrasODM, AR display in HoloPOCUS/UltrARsound), but no retrieved paper reports an end-to-end clinical evaluation of operator-guided gap filling with measured coverage gain.
- A coverage map is only as reliable as the pose estimate: with sensorless drift of 5-10 mm, a coverage map at 1-2 mm voxel resolution will mislabel gaps; the thesis's "overconfidence under localization noise" metric addresses exactly this.

### Gaps
- Quantitative results of the MIT thesis (coverage %, time savings) could not be extracted from the DSpace abstract page; the full PDF was not read.
- No paper using the literal term "next-best-view" for ultrasound coverage was found; robotic-ultrasound NBV work (e.g., RL-based scanning) was outside the retrieved results.
- HoloPOCUS tracking accuracy numbers were not available from the abstract page.

## Key Question 5: What hardware is typically needed, and is phone-camera (ARKit/ARCore) tracking of a probe accurate enough? Which papers tried it?

### Takeaway
Research-grade systems use an NDI Polaris (optical, ~0.25 mm) or Aurora/trakSTAR (EM, ~0.5-1.4 mm) with a tracked rigid body or 6-DOF sensor on the probe, N-wire spatial calibration and temporal calibration through PLUS, and a 20-30 fps frame grab; low-cost alternatives are a probe-mounted IMU (WT901C, BNO055), a probe-mounted stereo/mono camera (VIO/SLAM, 1.5-4 mm), or an external RGB-D camera (0.9-3 mm). No peer-reviewed study was found that tracks a real probe with iPhone ARKit for reconstruction; the one smartphone study (ARCore, 2019) targeted a simulator and found it "less reliable and accurate" than a Vive tracker, while standalone ARKit benchmarks show ~0.02 m/s relative drift, i.e., too large for mm-level volume assembly without image-based correction.

### Cited Findings
- Reference hardware in TUS-REC2024: Ultrasonix scanner, 4DC7-3/40 curvilinear probe, NDI Polaris Vicra (<= 0.25 mm RMS), PLUS Toolkit recording, 20 fps — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2). MoNetV2: Mindray M8 + L12-4s, WT901C-232 IMU, trakSTAR EM, 30 fps — [arXiv 2506.15835](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1). Prevost: Stryker NAV3 optical — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Calibration: PLUS "now offers automatic spatial and temporal calibration methods" — [Lasso et al. 2014](https://labs.cs.queensu.ca/perklab/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/02/Lasso2014a-manuscript.pdf); MLRecon used N-wire phantom spatial calibration and cross-correlation temporal calibration — [arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- Smartphone ARCore vs HTC Vive tracker for a virtual probe (Jann, Reinhold, Teistler, MuC 2019): ARCore 6DoF tracking "is generally less reliable and accurate than the HTC Vive tracker" but "can be used as a simple and acceptable solution for controlling a virtual ultrasound probe" (training simulator, not real scanning) — [HS Flensburg paper](https://hs-flensburg.de/sites/default/files/2022-02/muc2019_handyalssonde_paper.pdf).
- Standalone ARKit VIO benchmark (iPhone 12 Pro Max): "the most stable and high accuracy/consistency" of four proprietary systems, relative pose drift "about 0.02 m per second"; VIO is identical across iPhone/iPad models — [PMC9785098](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9785098/); [arXiv 2207.06780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.06780). Outdoors ARKit "struggles with obtaining reliable depth information", with path-length errors of ~1.9 m to >30 m over 168 m depending on device — [search summary](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/structure-reports-capture-accurate-3d-building-models-iphone/).
- Probe-mounted camera VIO with a wireless handheld scanner: bone reconstruction error 1.25 mm (VIO) vs 1.21 mm (motion capture) — [arXiv 2404.15847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15847); Intel RealSense T265 VIO for 3D US/photoacoustic: pose SD ~0.13-0.2 mm — [PMC10278605](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10278605/).
- Probe-mounted binocular camera + IMU (UKF on Lie groups): 3.78 mm / 0.36 deg — [bohrium](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/19782632). Probe-mounted mono cameras + textured mat: 1.46-1.98 mm APE — [arXiv 2510.15668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15668). Stereo VIO (Buchanan 2024): ~4.0 mm APE — [MLRecon](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- External commodity cameras: Orbbec Astra 2 RGB-D + FoundationPose/SAM 2: 0.88-1.85 mm, 0.48-1.33 deg, 140 Hz on a laptop GPU — [arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1); RealSense D435i RGB + ArUco dodecahedron: 2.78-2.86 mm mean error — [PMC11275361](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11275361/); RGB-D + 3D-printed marker: 5.6-5.9 mm — [deepai](https://deepai.org/publication/a-novel-augmented-reality-ultrasound-framework-using-an-rgb-d-camera-and-a-3d-printed-marker); authors note "potential democratization of RGB-D cameras integrated in mobile devices and AR glasses" — same source.
- Headset tracking: HoloLens 2 depth camera + retroreflective spheres 1.98 mm / 1.81 deg static, 2.81 mm / 1.70 deg dynamic — [PMC9515035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9515035/).
- Low-cost mechanical/optical alternatives: optical-mouse sensor tracking 0.32-0.46 mm RMS surface error on phantom — [WPI thesis](https://digitalcommons.wpi.edu/etd-theses/107); gimbal + distance sensors + IMU add-on (2020) that could estimate and compensate pressure deformation — [PMC7699245](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7699245/).
- Inside-out probe-camera methods "remain susceptible to incremental drift over extended trajectories" — [MLRecon](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).

### Inferences
- A phone rigidly mounted on the probe running ARKit/ARCore is architecturally the same as the probe-mounted VIO systems above (camera + IMU, inside-out), which achieved 1.2-4 mm; the ARKit drift figure of ~2 cm/s relative error suggests raw ARKit poses would need image-based (speckle) refinement or loop closure to reach mm accuracy over a 10-20 s sweep. This is an inference; no paper measured ARKit on a real probe.
- An external phone/tablet camera observing the probe (outside-in, MLRecon-style) is the more promising phone route because foundation-model 6D tracking of a CAD-known rigid object reached ~1 mm, but it needs a depth stream and a GPU.
- Hardware minimum for a research-grade guided system today: a clinical probe with a frame grabber or research API (Clarius/Butterfly/Ultrasonix), a pose source (optical tracker for GT; camera or IMU for deployment), PLUS/Slicer for calibration and compounding, and a laptop GPU for DL pose estimation.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed ARKit-based real-probe tracking study for 3D US reconstruction was found; ARCore evidence is limited to a 2019 simulator paper without numeric error.
- Cost figures for the low-cost camera systems were not reported in the retrieved papers.

## Key Question 6: Which open-source (and key commercial) toolkits exist?

### Takeaway
PLUS Toolkit (BSD-3, Queen's PerkLab) plus 3D Slicer's SlicerIGT extension form the standard open pipeline for tracked acquisition, spatial/temporal calibration and real-time volume reconstruction; Stradx/Stradwin/Stradview (Cambridge, Treece) is the classic academic freehand 3D US package with pressure-correction; ImFusion (commercial, free academic SDK grant) provides compounding/registration and now leads sensorless research (DualTrack); recent DL methods publish code on GitHub (TUS-REC baselines, UltrasODM, DualTrack, UltraGauss on publication).

### Cited Findings
- PLUS: "Volume Reconstruction: 3D volume reconstruction from tracked ultrasound images"; a standalone VolumeReconstructor application; reconstruction "real-time, during image acquisition. Supports various interpolation and compounding methods"; "automatic spatial and temporal calibration methods"; BSD-3-Clause; primary citation Lasso et al., IEEE TBME 2014; descended from the SynchroGrab library (2008); talks to 3D Slicer via OpenIGTLink; supports 58+ devices per current docs — [PlusLib docs](https://pluslib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/); [citation page](https://pluslib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/about/citation.html); [Lasso 2014](https://labs.cs.queensu.ca/perklab/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/02/Lasso2014a-manuscript.pdf).
- SlicerIGT: "based on the PLUS library and the 3D Slicer application framework ... accessible to users from the 3D Slicer extension manager. Most applications do not require programming" — [PMC4437531](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4437531/); [Ungi 2013](https://labs.cs.queensu.ca/perklab/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/02/Ungi2013c.pdf).
- TUS-REC used PLUS to record the Polaris Vicra stream — [TUS-REC2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Stradx (Cambridge, Prager/Gee/Treece): works with NDI Polaris and two magnetic sensors, rapid calibration (Prager et al. 1998), pressure deformation "corrected by image correlation, then anchored to the sensor's measured positions" — [Cambridge Treece group page](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/Main/GMT_3dus).
- ImFusion: ImFusion Suite (visual interface for the SDK), ImFusion SDK (framework with extensions), ImFusion Labels; free SDK access through a software grant for universities, hospitals and non-commercial researchers; DualTrack (TUS-REC2025 winner) code on GitHub — [imfusion.com](https://www.imfusion.com/). ImFusion staff (Zettinig, Salehi, Prevost, Wein) authored the 2018 "Recent Advances in Point-of-Care Ultrasound Using the ImFusion Suite for Real-Time Image Analysis" workshop paper and the VCBM 2018 multi-sweep decompression/stitching paper — [bibsonomy](https://www.bibsonomy.org/bibtex/18f1e498718e0d34911b9ca8c7ca50ba); [EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/vcbm20181223).
- Challenge/DL code: TUS-REC2025 baseline and evaluation code github.com/QiLi111/TUS-REC2025-Challenge_baseline — [TUS-REC2025 site](https://github-pages.ucl.ac.uk/tus-rec-challenge/); UCL long-term-dependency and nonrigid papers state code/data public — [arXiv 2310.10248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10248), [arXiv 2407.05767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05767); UltrasODM github.com/AnandMayank/UltrasODM — [PMLR v317](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v317/anand26a.html); UltraGauss CUDA code "released upon publication" (ICLR 2026) — [arXiv 2505.05643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05643); Ultra-NeRF released tracked-sweep datasets — [PMLR v227](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v227/wysocki24a.html).
- Compounding algorithms: forward vs backward (voxel-based) reconstruction; a hybrid GPU method reconstructs a 256^3 volume in 0.35 s (simple interpolation) or 0.82 s (advanced), speed-ups of 197x/84x (Karamalis et al., SPIE 2009) — [TUM CAMP pdf](https://www.campar.in.tum.de/pub/karamalis2009spie/karamalis2009spie.pdf); classical review of reconstruction algorithms: Solberg et al., UMB 2007, 33:991-1009 — [SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/1273127).
- Reviews: Mozaffari & Lee, "Freehand 3-D ultrasound imaging: a systematic review", UMB 2017 43(10); Adriaans et al., "Trackerless 3D Freehand Ultrasound Reconstruction: A Review", Applied Sciences Sept 2024 (doi 10.3390/app14177991; searched PubMed, Scopus, IEEE Xplore) — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/17/7991/htm); [preprints.org](https://preprints.org/manuscript/202408.1319/v1).

### Inferences
- For a prototype guided-mapping system, PLUS + SlicerIGT already give tracked acquisition, calibration, live compounding and a scripting layer (Python in Slicer), so the novel engineering is the pose source (camera/IMU/DL) and the coverage logic, not the compounding.

### Gaps
- Stradwin/Stradview current version, licence and download status were not retrievable (the Cambridge page retrieved describes the older Stradx).
- ImFusion product documentation for sweep stitching/sensorless features (PIUR integration) could not be fetched; only the homepage was readable.
- The Adriaans 2024 review full text was blocked (HTTP 403), so its own summary tables were not captured.

## Key Question 7 (supplementary scope): Volume representations, implicit neural / NeRF / Gaussian-splatting reconstruction, ultrasound SLAM, and multi-sweep compounding/stitching

### Takeaway
Since 2023 a parallel line of work replaces voxel compounding with physics-aware implicit representations (Ultra-NeRF 2023, UlRe-NeRF 2024, AIA-UltraNeRF 2025) and Gaussian splatting (UltraGauss ICLR 2026, UltraGS 2025, an unsupervised transvaginal GS method 2025), which need tracked poses or jointly estimate them and mainly target novel-view synthesis quality rather than pose accuracy; multi-sweep stitching remains dominated by registration plus probe-pressure "decompression", and true loop-closure SLAM for freehand US is rare (EchoFusion 2018; start/end loop-closure priors in 2024 VIO work).

### Cited Findings
- Ultra-NeRF (Wysocki, Azampour, Eilers, Busam, Salehi, Navab; MIDL 2023, PMLR v227 pp. 382-401): "physics-enhanced implicit neural representation" with ray-tracing-based neural rendering modelling direction-dependent appearance; data: simulated liver B-mode sweeps and spine-phantom sweeps acquired with a robotic arm; datasets released — [PMLR v227](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v227/wysocki24a.html). Reported SSIM 0.45-0.54, no drift reported — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2). UlRe-NeRF describes Ultra-NeRF as mapping 3D coordinates to five physical parameters through a coordinate-based MLP and adds a directional MLP for view-dependent reflection — [arXiv 2408.00860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00860).
- AIA-UltraNeRF (arXiv 2511.18293): acoustic-impedance-aware NeRF with hash encodings for robotic US reconstruction and localization, inference 9.9x faster than vanilla NeRF — [arXiv 2511.18293](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18293).
- UltraGauss (Eid, Namburete, Henriques, Oxford; arXiv May 2025, ICLR 2026): "first ultrasound-specific Gaussian Splatting framework", models probe-plane intersections in 3D, state-of-the-art reconstructions in ~5 min and 0.99 SSIM within 20 min on one GPU on real clinical data; clinicians rated it most realistic — [arXiv 2505.05643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05643); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011906).
- UltraGS (arXiv 2511.07743): real-time, sensorless-freehand-oriented GS with depth-aware Gaussians, learnable fields of view and a differentiable acoustic approximation — [arXiv 2511.07743](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07743v2). Unsupervised transvaginal pelvic reconstruction (arXiv 2508.14552): anisotropic 3D Gaussians optimised from image-level supervision "with sensorless probe motion estimation", no external tracking or learned pose estimator — [arXiv 2508.14552](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14552v1).
- Wein et al. (thyroid, 180 sweeps, 9 volunteers, optical GT): 54-DOF trajectory refinement with relative trajectory error 0.16 +/- 0.09, Dice 0.66 +/- 0.12 — registration-driven refinement of tracked sweeps — [TUS-REC2024 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2).
- Multi-sweep compounding: registration "can be achieved using image registration if the offset is small and assumptions about the spatial arrangement of the volumes hold, e.g., when performing an imaging sweep at constant speed"; otherwise "external tracking such as electromagnetic or optical tracking has to be used" — [EchoFusion, arXiv 1807.10583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10583). Decompression model with "domain knowledge of the global acquisition pattern for regularization ... allows for seamless stitching of multiple overlapping 3D freehand sweeps into one volume" (Schulte zu Berge et al., VCBM 2018) — [EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/vcbm20181223). Patent approach: reconstruct each sweep separately then fuse overlapping voxels by confidence-weighted average — [US 11717268](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11717268); patent aiming to minimise the number of stitching seams — [US 20190336110](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20190336110); confidence-weighted 3D reconstruction — [US 9700284](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9700284).
- Ultrasound SLAM: EchoFusion (Khanal et al. 2018) uses a Residual 3D U-Net for tracking semantics fed to SLAM, yielding "relative transformations between subsequent volumes, surface reconstruction of the target anatomy, and reconstruction of a compounded volume at the same time", for moving fetuses — [arXiv 1807.10583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10583); an intracardiac echo system used a probabilistic SLAM framework to mosaic 2D ICE images into 3D but relied on a pose sensor for initialisation — [search summary](https://alumni-friends.as.uky.edu/bibcite/reference/751); loop-closure priors between scan start and end reduced IMU-only APE/RPE but remained worse than VIO — [arXiv 2404.15847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15847).
- Photoacoustic + US freehand DL reconstruction (2025) extends the sensorless paradigm to dual modality — [arXiv 2502.03505](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03505v1).

### Inferences
- INR/GS methods are complementary to pose estimation rather than replacements: they assume poses (from tracker or from a sensorless network) and give a differentiable volume that could in principle supply photometric loop-closure constraints, but no retrieved paper demonstrates drift reduction from this on in vivo freehand sweeps.
- Loop closure in freehand US lacks repeatable landmarks; the practical substitutes used so far are external constraints (camera, IMU, start/end priors) or anatomical-shape priors (segmentation-driven registration, as in Wein's trajectory refinement).

### Gaps
- No quantitative pose-accuracy results for Ultra-NeRF/UltraGauss-style joint pose refinement on freehand in vivo sweeps were found.
- Whether UltraGauss requires tracked poses as input could not be confirmed from the abstract.
- I found no freehand-US paper with explicit anatomy-based loop-closure detection and pose-graph optimisation; "ultrasound SLAM" results are limited to EchoFusion (fetal, 2018) and sensor-assisted systems.

## Summary of what is still unsolved (cross-cutting, with sources)
- Sensorless global drift of 5-10 mm on forearm and >13 mm on harder protocols remains above clinical needs; "not yet solved to a clinically satisfactory degree" — [TUS-REC2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2); ">13 mm drift" US-only — [arXiv 2607.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12398).
- Generalisation across scan length, scanning protocol (rotation/tilt, S-shapes) and anatomy is the main failure mode; benchmarks are forearm-only — [TUS-REC2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21765v2); DualTrack "liver or heart scans need future study" — [arXiv 2509.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09530v1).
- Out-of-plane motion estimation and nonrigid tissue deformation under probe pressure remain the physical bottlenecks — [MoNetV2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15835v1); [arXiv 2407.05767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05767).
- IMU-only tracking is impractical; cameras (probe-mounted or external) are the realistic low-cost route to ~1-4 mm — [arXiv 2404.15847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15847); [arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1).
- Interactive coverage guidance exists only as phantom/simulation prototypes with no clinical validation — [MIT thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/169967).
- Foundation models help as tracking backbones for external cameras (FoundationPose, SAM 2) but did not beat a plain CNN as the ultrasound-image backbone in DualTrack (USFM, MedSAM, iBOT tested) — [arXiv 2603.00990](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00990v1); [arXiv 2509.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09530v1).
