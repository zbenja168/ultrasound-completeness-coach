# Does the "dental 3D imaging from multiple views" analogy transfer to ultrasound? Physics and engineering differences (as of October 2026)

Scope note: These notes cover (1) how dental 3D imaging (CBCT, panoramic, intraoral optical scanners) actually works and which of those is a true analog for "software that guides a user to fill in a 3D map"; (2) the physics that make ultrasound fundamentally different from x-ray and optical scanning for volumetric reconstruction; (3) whether a complete 3D volume is even the right goal in ultrasound versus the "right standard plane" framing; (4) existing coverage/completeness concepts in ultrasound; and (5) feasibility of consumer-device (iPhone/AR headset) probe tracking. Research was conducted via web search and fetch in October 2026; roughly 40 tool calls. Where a fetch failed or a figure could only be retrieved from a search snippet rather than full text, that is flagged.

---

## Key Question 1: How does dental 3D imaging actually work, and which modality is the true analog for "guide the user to fill out a 3D map"?

### Takeaway
CBCT and panoramic radiography are *machine-controlled, transmissive, single-pass* acquisitions with no user-in-the-loop coverage feedback; they are not analogs of a guided freehand mapping app. The intraoral optical scanner (3Shape TRIOS, Align iTero) is the closest analog: a handheld optical sensor stitches thousands of frames into a surface mesh in real time and the on-screen model itself is the coverage map, with newer iTero software explicitly flagging "areas that require additional scans." Consumer photogrammetry (Apple Object Capture's coverage dial, Polycam Guided Mode's live point cloud) and robotics "next-best-view" planning are the same idea in non-dental form.

### Cited Findings

**Cone-beam CT (CBCT): rotating source + detector, tomographic reconstruction, no user guidance**
- CBCT uses a divergent pyramid- or cone-shaped x-ray beam aimed at the region of interest; "the x-ray source and detector rotate about the area of interest, capturing multiple projection images in a single rotation of the gantry," in contrast to conventional CT's fan beam and helical progression that requires multiple gantry passes — [SEDENTEXCT technical description of CBCT](https://www.sedentexct.eu/content/technical-description-cbct); [Radiodontics: CBCT, what is it and how does it work](https://www.radiodontics.com/cbct-what-is-it-and-how-does-it-work/)
- Only one rotation or less is needed to acquire a full 3D dataset; the detector's 2D projections "are then compiled by the computer into volumetric data" displayed as multiplanar slices or 3D renderings — [SEDENTEXCT](https://www.sedentexct.eu/content/technical-description-cbct)
- The reconstruction is based on the cone-beam (Feldkamp) algorithm published in 1984 — [Sri Venkateshwaraa dental CBCT chapter (PDF)](https://svdcpondy.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/54-Cone-Beam-Computer-Tomography.pdf)
- A Columbia tech-transfer listing describes "panoramic CBCT" that obtains full-scan CT images using only a half-scan rotation, confirming the acquisition geometry is fixed by the machine, not by an operator sweeping — [Columbia Technology Ventures](https://inventions.techventures.columbia.edu/technologies/panoramic-cone-beam-computed--2934)

**Panoramic radiography: moving slit beam, focal trough**
- Panoramic imaging generates "a long thin slit X-ray beam from the X-ray source in a direction parallel to the rotation axis"; collimator opening shape determines whether a cone beam (rectangular/round opening) or a slit beam (narrow opening) is produced, and some units switch between the two by moving a slit in front of a single source — [US Patent 6,493,415 (X-ray computed tomography method and apparatus)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6493415)
- In slit/rotational tomography, structures in the image layer stay sharp while structures above and below blur; the sharp zone is called the "focal trough or image layer or zone of sharpness" (secondary/flashcard source; treat as a weak citation) — [Brainscape Xray Outcome 8](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/xray-outcome-8-16787108/packs/22325020)
- Hybrid dental units have a panoramic mode that rotates a source and flat-panel detector on a circular path plus cephalometric modes — [US Patent 12,727,838 (Oral cone beam X-ray imaging system)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12727838)

**Intraoral optical scanners: handheld, real-time stitched surface mesh, operator fills gaps**
- 3Shape's own explainer: the scanner works "by projecting light onto the surfaces of the teeth and recording the reflected data"; it "captures thousands of images in quick succession from different angles and positions"; "the system aligns and stitches the images together to create a continuous representation of the scanned area"; the software converts image data into point clouds and surface geometry, updates the model as new data arrives, and the model is built in real time "allowing the clinician to see the captured areas and identify any missing data." The page does not describe hole color-coding — [3Shape: How does an intraoral scanner work](https://www.3shape.com/blog/digital-dentistry/how-intraoral-scanner-works)
- Optical principles used: structured light (known pattern projected, deformation recorded by sensors), confocal imaging (multiple focal depths), and triangulation (angles between light source, object, sensor); polarization filters favor tooth-surface signal over subsurface enamel scatter — [3Shape](https://www.3shape.com/blog/digital-dentistry/how-intraoral-scanner-works); [Dental Update: Intraoral scanners in dentistry](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2018/10/01/intraoral-scanners-in-dentistry-an-update-on-digital-technology/)
- A 2020 review of 24 studies found active triangulation "offers the highest trueness" and that parallel confocal technology does not require a set focusing distance so it "can produce accurate images even when the tip touches the teeth"; restoration type, preparation outline, scanning technology and powder application all affect IOS accuracy — [Review of intraoral scanner accuracy (PMC7008627)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7008627/)
- A Czech 2025 review states accuracy strategies "primarily involve optimizing the scanning path and incorporating additional devices to avoid merging errors" — i.e., scan path/sweep strategy is a first-order accuracy factor even for optical scanning — [Principy intraorálních skenerů (cspzl.dent.cz PDF)](https://cspzl.dent.cz/pdfs/sto/2025/01/03.pdf)
- 3Shape TRIOS (2012-2013 launch coverage): "live 3-D visualization and motion-sensor interface enables dentists to view the digital impression being built on their touch screen during scanning"; it "combines hundreds or thousands of 3-D pictures to create the final 3-D digital impression based on real data rather than interpolated artificial surfaces" — [Dentistry IQ: 3Shape launches TRIOS Color](https://www.dentistryiq.com/front-office/software/article/16353099/3shape-launches-trios-color-digital-impression-solution); [Aegis Dental Network: 3Shape TRIOS](https://aegisdentalnetwork.com/id/2012/08/3shape-trios)
- Align iTero Element software: "Real-time scan feedback also is now available to provide guidance to areas that require additional scans before processing the case. This guidance saves time and prevents the need to rescan due to missing data, according to the company" — [Dentistry Today (Align iTero Element update)](https://dentistrytoday.com/?p=49377)
- iTero display: a viewfinder on the touchscreen "displays exactly what the camera is seeing alongside the model of what has been scanned" — [Dentaltown: Product Profile iTero](https://www.dentaltown.com/magazine/article/6719/product-profile-itero)
- Lab acceptance guidance for iTero: "Review for any areas that have not been scanned and rescan or fill any missed areas as needed" — the post-scan "fill the holes" step is an explicit part of the workflow — [DDS Lab iTero cheat sheet](https://blog.ddslab.com/cheat-sheet-for-itero-dental-impressions)

**Consumer photogrammetry / 3D-scanning apps: explicit coverage UX**
- Apple Object Capture for iOS (WWDC23): guided capture shows "a capture dial that indicates which areas of the object have adequate images"; the app marks regions needing more images with visual guidance; it automatically selects shots with good sharpness/exposure and collects LiDAR points from various angles; real-time feedback includes too-dark warnings, "move too fast" pauses, too-far/too-close text reminders, and an arrow symbol when the object leaves the view; `ObjectCaptureSession.Feedback` exposes states such as `.objectNotFlippable`, and `beginNewScanPass()` / `beginNewScanPassAfterFlip()` manage multi-pass capture; reconstruction runs on-device to USDZ — [Apple WWDC23 session 10191 "Meet Object Capture for iOS"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10191); [ObjectCaptureSession API docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession.md); [WWDC Notes summary](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-10191-meet-object-capture-for-ios/)
- Polycam Guided Mode (iOS Object Mode): "Points will appear in real-time, forming a point cloud of your object. You can rotate the point cloud during the scan to check your progress," and poor images can be deleted before processing — [Polycam Help: How to Use Guided Mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/35619450890132-How-to-Use-Guided-Mode-in-Polycam)
- Polycam coverage heuristics: 30-50% photo overlap for interiors, 80% for drone capture, 20-50 photos for simple objects and 100-200 for complex ones, capture every side including top — [Polycam interior photogrammetry tutorial](https://poly.cam/blog/high-resolution-interior-photogrammetry-full-walkthrough-polycam-tutorial); [Polycam drone photogrammetry](https://poly.cam/tools/drone-photogrammetry); [Polycam: Capture large objects](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/30549135578388-How-to-Capture-a-Large-Objects-in-Polycam)

**Robotics "next-best-view" (NBV): the formal version of "where to scan next"**
- Wu et al. 2014 use a per-point confidence map on the partially reconstructed surface to compute next-best-views for autonomous robot scanning, with the stated goal "to ensure the high quality scanning of the model rather than minimize the number of scans"; deployed on a PR2 and an industrial arm — [Wu et al., Quality-driven Poisson-guided autoscanning (Konstanz)](https://graphics.uni-konstanz.de/publikationen/Wu2014QualitydrivenPoisson/index.html)
- Foundational NBV formulations (Isler et al.) maximize volumetric information gain for active 3D reconstruction — cited in [Wu et al. page](https://graphics.uni-konstanz.de/publikationen/Wu2014QualitydrivenPoisson/index.html)
- A 2026 arXiv paper, RobOralScan, applies learned active scanning to *robotic intraoral scanning* — i.e., the dental scanner itself is now being framed as an NBV problem — [RobOralScan (arXiv 2606.26955)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26955)

### Inferences
- The user's mental model ("x-rays from multiple views build a 3D map") conflates two different dental technologies. CBCT is tomographic reconstruction from projections of a *transmissive* modality where every ray integrates attenuation through the whole head; coverage is guaranteed by the machine's fixed orbit, and no user feedback loop exists. The intraoral scanner is a *reflective surface* capture where coverage is NOT guaranteed and the user must fill holes; that is the real analog of a guided-mapping app.
- The intraoral scanner analogy works because (a) teeth are rigid and static, (b) enamel is a specular/diffuse optical surface that returns signal from essentially any viewing angle within the field of view, (c) the sensor-to-surface geometry is known from the structured-light/confocal optics so each frame is self-localizing against the growing mesh (SLAM-like registration), and (d) the target is a bounded 2D surface, so "complete" is well-defined (no holes). Each of these conditions fails or degrades in ultrasound (see Key Question 2).
- The common UX pattern across iTero, Object Capture, and Polycam is: the growing model *is* the coverage display, with gaps visible as holes/missing regions; secondary cues are a coverage dial, directional arrows, and speed/distance/lighting warnings. None of the official docs retrieved describe a color-coded "mesh completeness heatmap" per se; 3Shape and iTero describe seeing missing data and being guided to areas needing additional scans.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve a primary Align/iTero help-center page describing exactly how missing areas are rendered (color, outline, arrows); the only sources are trade press quoting Align. 3Shape's page confirms real-time model building for spotting missing data but does not describe hole color-coding.
- No Matterport documentation was retrieved in this research (searches did not surface it); its coverage UX could not be cited.
- Panoramic "focal trough" explanation came from a flashcard site; a textbook citation would be stronger.

## Key Question 2: Why does ultrasound differ fundamentally from x-ray and optical scanning for volumetric reconstruction, and which anatomy is suited or unsuited to a static "fill-in-the-volume" model?

### Takeaway
Ultrasound is a reflective, angle-dependent, attenuating, operator-setting-dependent modality acquired through a probe that deforms the tissue it images, in organs that move; each of these breaks an assumption that intraoral/photogrammetry scanning depends on (static rigid target, view-invariant surface appearance, no occlusion by the target's own materials). Static, superficial, bounded, breath-holdable structures (carotid, thyroid, breast, MSK tendons, bladder, liver with breath hold, prostate on a fixture) are the viable targets; heart, aerated lung, and gas-filled bowel are poorly suited and need gating or are physically opaque.

### Cited Findings

**Reflective, specular, and angle-dependent (anisotropy)**
- Specular reflection occurs at smooth interfaces much larger than the wavelength; "like a mirror reflecting light, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection... to maximize echo return, the beam must be perpendicular (90°) to the interface" — [Educato ARDMS SPI: Acoustic interactions](https://www.educato.com/app/ardms/library/lessons/spi-perform-ultrasound-examinations/spi-acoustic-interactions/8c138d7b-023c-4f51-a63b-f4c8b8926889)
- Anisotropy: "When the ultrasound beam is incident on a fibrillar structure such as a tendon or a ligament, the organized fibrils may reflect a majority of the insonating sound beam in a direction away from the transducer... the transducer does not receive the returning echo and assumes that the insonated area should be hypoechoic"; "the maximum return echo occurs when the ultrasound beam is perpendicular to the tendon"; the artifact "may prompt an incorrect diagnosis of tendinosis or tendon tear" — [Radiopaedia: Anisotropy (ultrasound)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/31805)
- Radiopaedia case: a normal Achilles tendon "may appear dark and tendinotic if it is insonated at less than 90 degrees"; the echo from a compact fibrillar structure "is highly dependent on the angle of incidence" — [Radiopaedia case: normal Achilles anisotropy](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/31817)
- Sonographers must use "heel-toe" or "rocking" movements to avoid misinterpreting angle-dependent changes — [Global Ultrasound Institute POCUSpedia: Anisotropy](https://globalultrasoundinstitute.com/pocuspedia/anisotropy)

**Attenuation and shadowing (bone, calcification, gas)**
- Acoustic shadowing is a region with little or no signal beyond a strongly reflecting or absorbing interface, because "all (or nearly all) of the transmitted sound wave" is reflected or absorbed; it occurs with bone or calcified gallstones and at interfaces with large impedance mismatch such as soft tissue/air; shadow intensity depends on focal depth ("the closer the depth of focus is to the object creating the shadow, the greater the shadow will be") — [Radiopaedia: Acoustic shadowing](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/acoustic-shadowing)
- Lung: "The high difference in acoustic impedance between superficial tissues and air in the lung prevents the penetration of the beam beneath the visceral pleura"; one source estimates "more than 96% of the US beam is reflected at tissue–chest wall/air-lung interface"; "Under anatomical conditions, it is not possible to visualize lung parenchyma"; lung ultrasound "images" are therefore artifacts (pleural line, A-lines from reverberation, B-lines when aeration falls) rather than anatomy. (Attribution between the two sources below is from search snippets; both discuss lung ultrasound physics.) — [Lung ultrasound physics review (PMC9732207)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9732207/); [The Ultrasound Journal (Mattioli) lung artefacts](https://www.mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/theultrasoundjournal/article/download/18495/13201/148319)
- A-lines are "repetitive horizontal echoic lines with equidistant intervals" equal to the skin-pleura distance, generated by reverberation between pleural line and transducer; a blurred pleural line arises "secondary to the tangential incidence of the ultrasound beam" — [PMC8774376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8774376)
- Robotic ultrasound literature: perpendicular-to-skin scans "create shadows below high-attenuation structures like bones due to acoustic shadowing, leading to an information loss in the scan" — [Sutedjo et al. 2022, Acoustic Shadowing Aware Robotic Ultrasound (FAU record; IEEE RA-L 7(2):1808-1815)](https://www.cris.fau.de/publications/290511260/)
- In confidence maps, "a lower confidence value of a pixel indicates an increased likelihood of acoustic shadow" — [Shadow-aware dual-agent spinal sonography (arXiv 2111.02167)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02167)

**Probe pressure deforms the anatomy**
- "To maintain good acoustic contact, the clinician must hold the probe against the subject's skin with some pressure: this inevitably deforms the anatomy beneath the probe"; "Unless the clinician maintains perfectly constant pressure throughout the scan, the different parts of the anatomy will be captured with different degrees of compression"; Cambridge's correction applies "a rigid in-plane translation and non-rigid depth compression... to each B-scan, so as to maximise the correlation between successive images," then a final affine transform so first/last frames match the external tracker; the image-based correction alone "may suffer from large-scale error accumulation" — [Treece, Cambridge 3D ultrasound (GMT_3dus)](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/Main/GMT_3dus)
- Freehand sweeps are therefore typically restricted to "either angular-swept motion, or linear trajectories perpendicular to the transducer's long side" to keep adjacent frames under similar compression — [Seeing Globally, Refining Locally (arXiv 2607.12398)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12398)

**Speckle, settings dependence, and operator dependence**
- Speckle decorrelation for image-based positioning "faces challenges because calibrated decorrelation curves are only valid for fully developed speckle, which is rare in real tissue scans"; purely image-based trajectory estimation shows "average trajectory drift exceeding 5 mm" — [arXiv 2607.12398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12398)
- Early sensorless methods struggled with "irregularly spaced frames, non-monotonic probe motion, and substantial in-plane movement" — [Housden et al. 2006, Sensorless reconstruction (summary via Liner)](https://liner.com/review/sensorless-reconstruction-freehand-3d-ultrasound-data)
- TUS-REC2024 "establishes the first benchmark for trackerless 3D freehand ultrasound reconstruction" with a public dataset and baseline — [TUS-REC2024 (arXiv 2506.21765)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21765v2)
- Reconstruction interpolation: pixel-based and distance-weighted methods "are sensitive to sampling density and speckle noise" and smooth object boundaries; B-scan pixel confidence varies with pixel location — [3D freehand US reconstruction via piecewise-smooth MRF (KAIST PDF)](https://sal.kaist.ac.kr/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/3D-US-CVIU.pdf)
- Gain: in a phantom study, gain adjustments above/below optimum "contributed to significantly different intra-system GSM values on 4/7 systems in near and 5/7 systems in far zone"; grey-scale median is reproducible only "from the same ultrasound system with similar gain settings" — [Grey-scale median gain dependence study (PMC6339613)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6339613)
- Carotid reproducibility was lower with high gain (grey-level saturation) and authors recommend keeping image depth "at the lowest attainable value" and documenting setup for follow-up — [Artery Research carotid setup study](https://arteryresearch.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.2991/artres.k.191127.001.pdf)
- Texture estimates depend on "the overall gain, Time-Gain-Compensation (TGC), Diffraction and Focusing, pre- and post-processing of the gray levels" — [ICEENG texture paper](https://iceeng.journals.ekb.eg/article_62329_6e085e521e2dcbb277c885cbfd3661b1.pdf)
- Fenster review: "US images suffer from shadowing, poor tissue–tissue contrast and image speckle"; 3D US resolution "will not be isotropic" and degrades with distance from the rotation axis — [Fenster, Parraga, Bax 2011, Interface Focus (PMC3262266)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262266)

**Motion: respiratory, cardiac, bowel**
- Acquisition must be fast "or gated to avoid image artefacts owing to involuntary, respiratory or cardiac motion"; "any motion during the scan will cause a mismatch in the acquired planes, resulting in the production of artefacts"; end-firing rotational scanning "is most sensitive to the motion of the transducer and the patient"; for prostate the authors recommend mounting the transducer on a fixture; a 4 cm carotid linear scan "can be collected in 6.7 s without cardiac gating" — [Fenster 2011 (PMC3262266)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262266)
- 3D echo guidelines (EAE/ASE 2012) discuss "3D gating and breath-hold" as acquisition challenges and advise acquiring from multiple transducer positions to overcome temporal/spatial resolution limits; gated 3D TEE modes should be used "whenever ECG and respiration gating requirements permit" — [AuntMinnie summary of EAE/ASE 3D echo guidelines](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ultrasound/article/15601501/european-us-societies-publish-3d-echo-guidelines); [ASE guideline page](https://www.asecho.org/guideline/3d-echo-2/)
- 2D-array (matrix) real-time 3D systems are limited to "about 2 or 3 volumes per second" and "few companies provide this technology" (as of the 2011 review) — [Fenster 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262266)

**Tracking-hardware limits for freehand 3D (relevant to any "map" approach)**
- Magnetic tracking: "electromagnetic interference can compromise the accuracy of the tracking"; nearby ferrous/conductive metals cause geometric distortion; probe-to-sensor calibration "must be calibrated accurately and precisely to avoid distortions"; sensorless 3D "does not guarantee that the three-dimensional US image is geometrically accurate" and "must not be used for measurements" — [Fenster 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262266)
- Optical tracking "requires an unobstructed line of sight"; EM tracking "is sensitive to nearby metal objects and electromagnetic interference" — [arXiv 2607.12398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12398)

**Anatomy suitability evidence**
- Well-suited: carotid (linear sweep, ~6.7 s, vessel-wall-volume quantification), prostate (fixture-mounted TRUS; 3D-guided biopsy), obstetrics (volume rendering used extensively) — [Fenster 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262266)
- Breast: automated breast ultrasound images each breast in three views (anteroposterior, medial, lateral) with a transducer on a compression plate, each view "up to about 300 2D images," and multiplanar reconstruction "can cover the entire breast from skin to chest wall" — [ABUS review (PMC6960315)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6960315); [ABUS state of the art 2020 (PDF)](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~bebis/CS791/Fall2023/Papers/OtherModalities/2020%20-%20Automatic%20breast%20ultrasound%20state%20of%20the%20art%20and%20future%20perspectives.pdf)
- Bladder: handheld devices now perform "fully automated 3D bladder volume measurements with minimal user input"; a 2025 prospective study compared Butterfly iQ handheld 3D vs 2D in 53 male volunteers against voided volume — [Handheld 3D vs 2D bladder volume study (PMC12428280)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12428280/); Butterfly iQ+ Bladder offers "walkthrough guidance for proper probe placement" and "3D rendering to confirm true bladder anatomy" — [Butterfly Network press release](https://www.butterflynetwork.com/press-releases/bladder-scanner)
- Poorly suited by physics: aerated lung (beam does not penetrate visceral pleura, >96% reflected) — [PMC9732207](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9732207/); heart (requires ECG/respiratory gating and multi-position acquisition) — [AuntMinnie EAE/ASE summary](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ultrasound/article/15601501/european-us-societies-publish-3d-echo-guidelines); bone-shadowed regions (ribs/spine) require shadow-aware pose optimization in robotic scanning — [Sutedjo 2022](https://www.cris.fau.de/publications/290511260/); [Tactile-guided intercostal robotic US (arXiv 2507.20282)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20282); kidney scans are "often blindly performed with excessive probe footprint, which frequently leads to acoustic shadowing and incomplete organ coverage" — [arXiv 2602.16641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16641)

### Inferences
- Mapping the dental assumptions onto ultrasound: (1) *Rigid static target* -> fails for heart/lung/bowel, holds approximately for thyroid, carotid, tendons, bladder, and liver under breath hold; (2) *View-invariant surface appearance* -> fails: a tendon or vessel wall can be bright at 90° and black at 80° (anisotropy/specular), so two frames of the same voxel from different angles legitimately disagree, and the compounded volume contains "holes" that are artifacts of angle, not of coverage; (3) *No self-occlusion by the target* -> fails: bone, calcification and gas cast shadows that cannot be filled from any angle on that side, so a "complete" volume is physically unattainable behind ribs or bowel gas; (4) *Sensor pose is self-registering* -> partially fails: speckle-based self-localization drifts >5 mm and is invalid outside fully developed speckle, so an external pose source (tracker, IMU + camera) is needed, unlike an intraoral scanner that registers against its own rigid mesh; (5) *Target shape independent of the sensor* -> fails: probe pressure compresses tissue by a variable amount per frame, so the "map" is of a deformed object whose deformation changes with each frame.
- Consequence: a naive "voxel coverage" display (did the probe sweep over this voxel?) would be misleading in ultrasound because a swept voxel may still be uninformative (shadowed, anisotropic, over/under-gained). A useful ultrasound coverage map must be *confidence-weighted* (as in the Sutedjo/Konstanz confidence-map line of work), not binary.
- For the anatomies that are well-suited, the existing clinical solutions (ABUS, 3D carotid, fixture-mounted TRUS, Butterfly bladder sweep) all constrain the sweep geometry (compression plate, linear track, fan sweep) rather than allowing arbitrary freehand "painting," which is the opposite of the intraoral scanner's free-angle capture.

### Gaps
- Radiopaedia's "dirty shadowing" article returned HTTP 404; the clean-vs-dirty (bone vs gas) shadow distinction is not directly cited here, though lung-physics sources cover the gas case.
- No quantitative figure for typical tissue compression under probe pressure (mm) was retrieved; Treece's page confirms the phenomenon and the correction, not the magnitude.
- A current textbook citation (Hoskins; AIUM) was not retrieved; physics claims rest on Radiopaedia, ARDMS teaching material, and peer-reviewed engineering papers.

## Key Question 3: Does a 3D volume actually help diagnosis in ultrasound, and is the "right view" (standard plane) problem more important than the "complete volume" problem?

### Takeaway
3D ultrasound has proven value in specific niches (LV volumes and mitral valve in echo, breast screening via ABUS, OB surface/multiplanar views as a *complement*), but 2D remains the standard everywhere, and professional bodies and payers do not endorse 3D replacing 2D for routine screening. Meanwhile, the clinically validated AI products for novice guidance (Caption Guidance, ScanNav/SonoLyst) and the strongest ML literature (SonoNet, Madani) all target *standard-plane acquisition and quality*, not volume completeness, which suggests "did you capture the required views at diagnostic quality?" is the more clinically grounded framing.

### Cited Findings

**3D echo**
- 3D echo gives "more accurate, reproducible, and less variable left ventricular quantitation than 2D" because 2D methods "are inherently limited by geometric assumptions about 3D ventricular shape based on 2D slices" — [Current Cardiology Reports 2013 (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11886-013-0377-z); [PMC3324722](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3324722)
- The strongest clinical case is mitral valve: 3D provides etiology, mechanism, severity (EROA, regurgitant volume), reparability, and guides transcatheter procedures; en-face views allow "better appreciation of the severity and mechanisms of valve diseases" — [Springer Medicine review of 3D LV analysis](https://www.springermedicine.com/echocardiography/echocardiography/a-review-of-current-trends-in-three-dimensional-analysis-of-left/22865188)
- The 2012 EAE/ASE recommendations exist to provide "a practical guide on how to acquire, analyze, and display the various cardiac structures using 3D echocardiography, as well as limitations of the technique" — [ASE guideline page](https://www.asecho.org/guideline/3d-echo-2/); [ESC PDF](https://www.escardio.org/static_file/Escardio/Subspecialty/EACVI/position-papers/EAE-ASE-recommendation-image-acquisition-3D-echo.pdf)

**3D in obstetrics**
- In its early years "the diagnostic evidence for 3D ultrasound was not strong enough to convert scanning procedures from 2D to 3D"; a 2019 prospective study concluded 3D "does add value in the diagnosis of some types of foetal malformations" but "rather than representing an alternative, 3D ultrasound is complementary to the conventional 2D sonography"; "a broad implementation of 3D ultrasound as a screening test alone cannot be currently recommended" — [AuntMinnie: 3D/4D ultrasound matures](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ultrasound/article/15615938/3d-4d-ultrasound-matures-as-it-shows-clinical-value); [Infant journal review (PDF)](https://www.infantjournal.co.uk/pdf/inf_118_7412.pdf)
- A US payer medical policy notes 3D/4D limitations of "high dependence on operator experience, low reproducibility and exposure to a higher intensity power than used in 2D," and that "there remains some question about the value of 3-D, 4-D or 5-D imaging outside of the controlled clinical setting" — [Anthem medical policy on 3D/4D OB ultrasound](https://www.anthem.com/medpolicies/abc/active/mp_pw_a053265.html)

**Breast (ABUS vs handheld)**
- Hellgren et al. 2017: in 118 breasts recalled from screening, both ABUS and handheld reached 88% sensitivity (22/25); handheld was more specific (93.5% vs 89.2%); authors call for larger studies — [PubMed 27565633](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27565633/)
- RSNA 2019 abstract (Jia et al., >1,200 women): ABUS produced higher specificity, PPV and AUC than handheld; "ABUS is reproducible because its acquisition is operator-independent" — [AuntMinnie: ABUS delivers better diagnostic performance](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/article/15624882/abus-delivers-better-diagnostic-performance)
- Small dense-breast studies (n=42, n=62) show ABUS sensitivity 94-97% vs handheld 87-93%, with mixed specificity — [Cairo University study](https://mjcu.journals.ekb.eg/article_443853_e99cd0a1078774820ea9b4925809bc77.pdf); [Al-Kindy College Medical Journal 2022](https://jkmc.uobaghdad.edu.iq/index.php/MEDICAL/article/view/794)
- Note: ABUS's benefit is attributed to *operator-independent, standardized coverage* (compression plate, fixed three views), not to freehand volume painting — [PMC6960315](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6960315)

**Standard-plane detection and acquisition guidance (the alternative framing)**
- SonoNet (Baumgartner et al., IEEE TMI 2017) detects 13 fetal standard views in real time from freehand 2D ultrasound, covering every view that must be saved under the UK FASP mid-pregnancy guidelines plus common cardiac views, with F1 0.798 in a real-time detection experiment and 90.09% retrospective retrieval accuracy; localization uses only image-level labels — [SonoNet (PMC6051487)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6051487/); [arXiv 1612.05601](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05601); [Imperial BioMedIA software page](https://biomedia.doc.ic.ac.uk/software/sononet-for-real-time-fetal-standard-scan-plane-detection/)
- Madani et al. 2018 (npj Digital Medicine): CNN classifies 15 standard echo views; 97.8% accuracy among 12 video views; on single low-resolution still images 91.7% vs 70.2-84.0% for board-certified echocardiographers; authors frame view classification as "the essential first step toward fuller computer-assisted interpretation" — [Madani et al. (PMC6395045)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6395045/)
- Caption Guidance (FDA De Novo, Feb 2020; 510(k) K200755 cleared 2020-04-16): "provides prescriptive guidance to users on how to maneuver the ultrasound probe to acquire standard echocardiographic images and video clips of diagnostic quality"; gives "real-time feedback on potential image quality, can auto-capture video clips, and automatically saves the best video clip acquired from a particular view"; pivotal trial used registered nurses with no prior ultrasound experience — [FDA authorization coverage (BioSpace)](https://www.biospace.com/fda-authorizes-marketing-of-first-cardiac-ultrasound-software-that-uses-artificial-intelligence-to-guide-user); [510(k) K200755 (Innolitics)](https://510k.innolitics.com/device/K200755); [Cardiac Rhythm News](https://cardiacrhythmnews.com/ai-guided-cardiac-imaging-system-given-fda-authorisation/)
- ScanNav (Intelligent Ultrasound, piloted at St George's 2018) "evaluates more than 50 individual criteria to verify that the six views required by the NHS Fetal Anomaly Screening Programme are complete and fit for purpose," "instantly confirming that the images they save conform to protocol, meaning additional images can be taken straight away if required"; trained on >350,000 images — [Cardiff University news](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/news/view/1096089-cardiff-spin-out-pioneers-ai-ultrasound-scanner); [Intelligent Ultrasound pilot announcement](https://intelligentultrasound.com/?p=1710)
- ScanNav Assist powers GE's SonoLyst (Voluson SWIFT, Expert 22): "the world's first fully integrated AI tool that recognizes the 20 views recommended by the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynaecology mid-trimester practice guidelines"; SonoLystX "uses AI to compare the acquired image to standardized criteria" — [Intelligent Ultrasound: Real-time AI image analysis for obstetric ultrasound](https://www.intelligentultrasound.com/?p=1175)

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence of clinical value for 3D is where 3D *measurement* beats 2D geometric assumptions (LV volumes, mitral valve, bladder volume, carotid plaque volume) or where 3D *standardizes acquisition* (ABUS). There is little evidence that an operator-painted, freehand "complete volume" of an organ improves diagnosis over well-chosen 2D standard planes; the OB literature explicitly says 3D is complementary and should not replace 2D screening.
- The commercially successful, regulator-cleared guidance products (Caption, ScanNav/SonoLyst) solve a *checklist of standard views with quality criteria*, which is itself a form of "coverage" — coverage of the protocol's required planes, not of voxels. This framing sidesteps all the physics obstacles in Key Question 2 because each view is judged by its own diagnostic quality, angle and settings included.
- A hybrid is plausible for a novice-guidance app: use a coarse pose/anatomy model only to say "you are near the heart/liver; rotate toward X," while the completeness metric is the number of protocol views captured at acceptable quality.

### Gaps
- No recent (post-2020) systematic review directly comparing 3D vs 2D echo outcomes was found; evidence is from narrative reviews 2012-2020 and concerns accuracy/reproducibility, not patient outcomes.
- An ISUOG statement specifically on 3D vs 2D in obstetrics was not located.
- Caption Guidance's later corporate history (acquired by GE HealthCare in 2023 per general knowledge) was not verified in this research and is not cited.

## Key Question 4: Are there existing or proposed "coverage map" / scan-completeness concepts in ultrasound?

### Takeaway
Yes, but almost all are *protocol-zone checklists* (BLUE/12-zone lung, FAST windows, the FASP six views, ISUOG 20 views, ABUS three views) rather than voxel-coverage maps. True volumetric coverage/confidence maps exist only in robotic-ultrasound research (Sutedjo 2022, shadow-aware spinal/intercostal/kidney scanning, SonoSelect 2026), where they are used to plan probe poses that reduce shadow and raise coverage.

### Cited Findings
- BLUE protocol: the original description divided the chest into anterior, lateral and posterior zones on each side, each split into upper and lower halves, for 12 sites; current practice simplifies to two anterior BLUE points and one PLAPS point per side, with a staged sequence (<3 minutes) — [Lung ultrasound in the critically ill (PMC3895677)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3895677/); [WikEM BLUE protocol template](https://wikem.org/wiki/Template:BLUE_Protocol); [iEM BLUE protocol](https://iem-student.org/blue-protocol/)
- A critical-care study uses a 12-zone lung ultrasound protocol with additional scan points — [Critical Care 2021 figure](https://ccforum.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13054-021-03759-3/figures/1)
- Fetal anomaly: ScanNav checks the six FASP views against >50 criteria in real time; SonoLyst recognizes the 20 ISUOG mid-trimester views (see Key Question 3 citations) — [Cardiff](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/news/view/1096089-cardiff-spin-out-pioneers-ai-ultrasound-scanner); [Intelligent Ultrasound](https://www.intelligentultrasound.com/?p=1175)
- ABUS: three standard views (AP, medial, lateral), but "single scan is not enough to cover the whole breasts," large breasts may need five volumes, and "an optimal, operator-independent scanning technique with coverage of the entire breast has not been well investigated" — [ABUS small-breast coverage study / review (PMC6960315)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6960315)
- Robotic volumetric coverage: Sutedjo et al. 2022 optimize probe poses for "volume coverage of a region of interest and an acoustic shadowing reduction in this volume"; the coverage sweep "achieves higher coverage with the trade-off of more performed poses," and shadow reduction "consistently leads to a higher coverage and confidence of the volumes when applied after a random or perpendicular scan" — [FAU record, IEEE RA-L 2022](https://www.cris.fau.de/publications/290511260/)
- Confidence-driven probe control (Chatelain et al.) uses ultrasound confidence maps to servo the probe — [HAL hal-01551431](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01551431)
- A thesis aggregates per-image confidence maps "into a 3D confidence volume in physical coordinates, providing an interpretable estimate of signal reliability and highlighting shadow-affected regions" — [UNIMORE thesis](https://unitesi.unimore.it/handle/20.500.14251/5778)
- SonoSelect (2026, arXiv; note v3 withdrawn over an affiliation error): frames probe-view selection as sequential decision-making, fusing each 2D view into a 3D spatial memory and rewarding "organ coverage, lower reconstruction uncertainty, and less redundant scanning"; in simulation, kidney-cyst detection reached 54.56% kidney coverage and 35.13% cyst coverage; "not all probe views are equally informative" — [SonoSelect (arXiv 2604.05933)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05933)
- Tactile-guided intercostal robotic ultrasound detects gaps from "bone-induced acoustic shadows" and triggers a fan motion to recover the occluded region — [arXiv 2507.20282](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20282)
- 2026 kidney robotic scanning uses an exploratory scan then template-guided pivoting because blind scanning "frequently leads to acoustic shadowing and incomplete organ coverage" — [arXiv 2602.16641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16641)
- Butterfly iQ+ Bladder provides "walkthrough guidance for proper probe placement" before an automated 3D bladder volume — [Butterfly press release](https://www.butterflynetwork.com/press-releases/bladder-scanner)

### Inferences
- The ultrasound community's native notion of completeness is "all protocol windows/views acquired at adequate quality" — the BLUE points, FAST windows, FASP/ISUOG planes. The products that have reached clinics operationalize exactly that. Voxel-level coverage is a research concept confined to robot arms with calibrated trackers, and even there it is defined as *confidence-weighted coverage* to account for shadows.
- The ABUS coverage finding (three fixed views still miss tissue in large breasts, and optimal whole-breast coverage "has not been well investigated") shows that coverage completeness is an open problem even with a motorized, plate-compressed, operator-independent system, which bounds expectations for a freehand consumer approach.

### Gaps
- No commercial handheld-ultrasound app was found that displays a "scan completeness score" or 3D coverage heatmap to the user; Butterfly's bladder guidance and Caption/ScanNav view-checklists are the closest. A FAST-specific checklist citation was not retrieved (search returned BLUE material only).
- SonoSelect is simulation-only and its arXiv v3 is withdrawn; treat as an indication of research direction, not a validated result.

## Key Question 5: Consumer-device feasibility — can an iPhone (ARKit) or AR headset localize a probe to ~1 mm, and what has been published?

### Takeaway
Markerless ARKit/ARCore world tracking drifts at centimeter scale (reported 1.2-43.2 cm hologram drift depending on user action; ~0.02 m/s relative pose error in one benchmark), which is one to two orders of magnitude worse than the ~1 mm needed for metric freehand 3D ultrasound. Every published phone/headset-based ultrasound tracking system that reaches ~0.5-2 mm uses *fiducial markers* (ArUco, spheres) observed by the device camera rather than relying on the device's own SLAM pose; HoloLens 2 + 5 ArUco markers achieved 0.49 mm / 0.60° in HoloPOCUS, and a 2026 phone-based system (MARVUS) uses nine ArUco markers on the probe plus a patient reference marker set.

### Cited Findings

**ARKit/ARCore drift and accuracy**
- Duke study of markerless smartphone AR: measurement app accuracy 0.4 cm in 95% of cases; "mean drift ranged from 1.2 cm to 43.2 cm across six user actions"; ARKit had lower drift on 4 of 6 actions but ARCore did better on walk-away-and-return (4.8 cm vs 25.1 cm for ARKit); drift correlated with placement distance (Spearman 0.4) — [Here To Stay: Measuring Hologram Stability (arXiv 2109.14757)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14757); [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10358173)
- A 2022 Sensors comparison of four visual-inertial systems found ARKit "the most stable and high accuracy/consistency, with a relative pose error of about 0.02 m per second" (repository record; full methodology not checked) — [GIST scholar record](https://scholar.gist.ac.kr/handle/local/8635)
- An Apple Developer Forums thread (2026) reports a world-tracking drift regression on LiDAR-equipped devices under iOS 26.4+, anecdotal, no mm figure — [Apple Developer Forums thread 827240](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/827240)
- Earlier work used motion capture to compare localization error of ARCore, ARKit and HoloLens 1, noting that "no public data on tracking accuracy for markerless AR on these platforms had been released" at the time — [NSF PAR 10296638](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10296638)
- ARCore-tracked smartphone used as a *virtual* probe for ultrasound training (Jann, Reinhold, Teistler, MuC 2019): ARCore 6DoF tracking "is generally less reliable and accurate than the HTC Vive tracker" but acceptable for controlling a virtual probe — [HS Flensburg paper PDF](https://hs-flensburg.de/sites/default/files/2022-02/muc2019_handyalssonde_paper.pdf)

**Headset- and phone-camera-tracked probes (marker-based)**
- HoloPOCUS (HoloLens 2, MICCAI ASMUS 2023): ArUco fiducials on the probe tracked with the headset's stereo cameras at 30 Hz; ~0.49 mm translation / 0.60° rotation error with five markers, ~1.08 mm / 1.31° with two; compared baselines: depth/IR spheres 2.81 mm / 1.70°, stereo spheres 1.90 mm / 1.18°, single monocular marker 6.09 mm / 6.73°; pose estimation ~5.2 ms; authors note fiducial registration error is uncorrelated with overlay accuracy for MR navigation; phantom user study showed reduced navigation time (figures in search snippet from the paper's table; abstract page gives no numbers) — [HoloPOCUS (arXiv 2308.13823)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13823)
- UltrARsound (HoloLens 2, IJCARS 2022): probe tracked with the headset's depth camera, "eliminating the need for an external tracking system"; median accuracy 1.98 mm in the static setting with a Kalman filter (Springer full text was paywalled; figure from search snippet/UC3M record) — [UC3M research portal record](https://researchportal.uc3m.es/display/act553923); [Springer DOI 10.1007/s11548-022-02695-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11548-022-02695-z)
- MARVUS (2026 arXiv): standard mobile phone tracks a conventional 2D probe via *nine ArUco markers strapped to the probe*, four on a calibration phantom, and a patient reference marker set to correct coarse patient motion; pose via corner detection + PnP + RANSAC + 1€ filter (ARKit/ARCore/LiDAR not mentioned); calibration reproducibility 0.826 ± 0.447 mm; tracked frames form a textured point cloud, nodule segmented with on-device EdgeTAM, voxelized and meshed (marching cubes) for volume; in 8 experts on 12 phantoms, inter-operator variability fell from 0.630 ± 0.549 cm³ (two-plane ellipsoid) to 0.270 ± 0.189 cm³ (reconstruction) and 0.161 ± 0.132 cm³ (reconstruction + AR); phantom-only validation — [MARVUS (arXiv 2602.18500)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18500v1)
- RGB-D camera + 3D-printed marker probe tracking: target pose localization error 5.6-5.9 mm; authors suggest RGB-D cameras in mobile devices may make freehand 3D ultrasound more accessible — [arXiv 2205.04350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.04350)
- Low-cost computer-vision 6-DoF freehand 3D US for orthopedics: average error norm 2.858 mm (SD 5.534 mm) vs an infrared optical reference in the best case — [PMC11275361](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11275361/)
- Camera-plus-ultrasound fusion for freehand reconstruction (2026): motivated by image-only drift >5 mm and the cost/occlusion problems of optical/EM trackers — [arXiv 2607.12398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12398)
- Reference-grade optical tracking (NDI Polaris) is quoted at "precision of up to 0.01 mm" in Cambridge's freehand 3D setup — [Treece, Cambridge](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/Main/GMT_3dus)

### Inferences
- The ~1 mm requirement is plausible only with the phone camera looking at fiducials on the probe (and ideally a patient reference), i.e., using the phone as a marker-based optical tracker, not by mounting the phone on the probe and trusting ARKit's world pose. Phone-on-probe ARKit tracking faces both centimeter-scale drift and the fact that the camera would be pointed at the patient's skin/sheets (low-texture surfaces that trigger ARKit's limited-tracking states).
- Even marker-based phone tracking has only been validated on phantoms (MARVUS) and the headset results (HoloPOCUS 0.49 mm) are tracking errors, not reconstructed-volume errors; the authors of HoloPOCUS explicitly caution that tracking error does not predict overlay accuracy.
- For the user's concept, a realistic consumer pipeline is: phone on a stand viewing ArUco markers on the probe + a patient reference marker; IMU on the probe for orientation smoothing; a confidence-weighted (not binary) coverage display; restricted to static superficial targets (thyroid, breast lump, tendon, bladder, carotid); and completeness defined by protocol views as much as by volume.

### Gaps
- Apple's own ARKit documentation pages ("Understanding World Tracking", `ARCamera.TrackingState.Reason`) could not be fetched (pages returned title only), so Apple's first-party statements on drift and limited-tracking reasons (initializing, excessive motion, insufficient features, relocalizing) are not quoted here; they are well known API cases but should be cited from the docs directly.
- No published study of an *iPhone physically mounted on an ultrasound probe* using ARKit visual-inertial odometry for 3D reconstruction was found; the closest are ARCore virtual-probe training (2019) and marker-based phone tracking (MARVUS 2026).
- No end-to-end phantom study reporting reconstructed *volume* geometric error for a HoloLens-tracked freehand sweep was found; only tracking errors.
- Butterfly's bladder 3D method (fan sweep vs IMU) could not be confirmed from public documentation.
