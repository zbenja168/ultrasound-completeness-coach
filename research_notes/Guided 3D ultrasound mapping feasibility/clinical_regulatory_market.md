# Clinical, Regulatory, Educational, and Market Context for Guided Novice Ultrasound Acquisition (with 3D mapping) — as of 8 October 2026

Scope note: US-focused for regulation/reimbursement; LMIC evidence included where it bears on the opportunity. All figures carry dates. Where a figure came only from trade press or vendor material rather than a primary source, that is flagged.

---

## KQ1. Quantitative evidence on ultrasound operator dependence, by exam type

### Takeaway
Operator dependence is real and measurable but very uneven across exams: FAST, DVT compression, and lung B-lines show large experience gradients in *sensitivity* (e.g., EP trainees 77% vs specialists 93% for DVT); echo and OB biometry show moderate but clinically meaningful *measurement* variability (7–8 EF points inter-reader; ±5–11% inter-observer limits of agreement for fetal AC/FL); thyroid and MSK show the weakest agreement on subjective descriptors (margins, tendon lesions; kappa as low as 0.08–0.30). The exams most dependent on *finding the right view* (echo, OB, FAST) are the ones where AI guidance and blind-sweep protocols have been proven; thyroid is where a freehand 3D-sweep product has already been cleared on the promise of "up to 80% less operator variability" (vendor claim).

### Cited Findings

**Echocardiography (view-finding + measurement)**
- Expert visual ("eyeball") LVEF estimation: inter-observer variability 7.5% (2D) and 8.4% (triplane) vs 2.3% for quantitative 3D-EF; intra-observer 3.8%/3.2% (30 patients, 3D echo reference, 2009) — [Cardiovascular Ultrasound 2009](https://cardiovascularultrasound.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1476-7120-7-41)
- A separate 2-reader study found much tighter visual-EF agreement (ICC 0.953, 95% CI 0.939–0.965), so inter-reader spread depends heavily on reader pool — [J Saudi Heart Assoc 2018](https://doaj.org/article/676a61c7bb77443aa98c4ecdfa130f86)
- In the Caption pivotal trial, the view where novices failed most was the subcostal IVC (57.4% diagnostic for nurses vs 91.5% for sonographers) and tricuspid valve (83.0%) — i.e., acquisition difficulty concentrates in specific windows — [Narang et al., JAMA Cardiol 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8204203/)

**Obstetric biometry (measurement reproducibility)**
- INTERGROWTH-21st (Sarris et al.; 175 scans, 140 fetuses, 3 experienced sonographers): inter-observer 95% limits of agreement ±4.9% (HC), ±8.8% (AC), ±11.1% (FL); intra-observer ±3.0%/±5.3%/±6.6%. Variability in mm grows with gestation but is constant as a percentage/Z-score — [Sarris et al., UOG (AKU repository)](https://ecommons.aku.edu/eastafrica_fhs_mc_obstet_gynaecol/201)
- Large QC follow-up (Cavallaro et al. 2017; 20,313 images, 4,321 fetuses, 8 sites): inter-observer limits ±4.4% (HC), ±6.0% (AC), ±5.6% (FL); QC flagged 3 of 31 sonographers for excessive variability and retraining — [Cavallaro et al. 2017, UOG (SGUL open access)](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/109161/1/Cavallaro_et_al-2017-Ultrasound_in_Obstetrics_%26amp%3B_Gynecology.pdf)
- Standard sonographer biometry GA error (MAE) in the UNC/Zambia dataset was 4.7 days overall and 5.4 days when untrained users scanned on low-cost devices — a direct measure of how much operator skill and hardware degrade the clinical-standard measurement — [Pokaprakarn et al. preprint (medRxiv listing)](https://medrxiv.org/highwire/citation/439638/medlars)

**FAST**
- Published FAST sensitivity ranges 75–100% and specificity 88–100% across studies; accuracy "is known to depend on physician experience" (Ma et al. 2005 compared EPs averaging ~394 vs ~47 career FAST exams, but kappa values were not retrievable from the abstract) — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4706728); [em-consulte abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/it/article/509117/kappa-values-for-focused-abdominal-sonography-for-)
- Junior vs senior residents, 300 blunt-trauma patients: 78.9% of junior-positive exams and 99.6% of junior-negative exams were confirmed by a senior — [Muhammad et al., Cureus 2018 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6726416/)
- EM vs radiology residents: kappa 0.789 (substantial), specificity 98.6% — [AJMS 2023 (Zenodo)](https://zenodo.org/records/10901786)
- Even expert *QA grading* of FAST image quality shows only slight inter-rater agreement (Fleiss kappa), meaning "diagnostic quality" is itself a noisy label — [GWU cross-sectional study](https://hsrc.himmelfarb.gwu.edu/gwhpubs/7553)

**DVT (compression ultrasound)**
- Systematic review of EP-performed DVT POCUS (15 studies, 2,511 exams): pooled sensitivity 90% (95% CI 82–95), specificity 95% (91–97). **EP specialists 93% sensitivity vs trainees 77% (CI 60–94)**; specificity 97% vs 87%. 3-point compression more sensitive than 2-point only among specialists — [Hercz et al., WestJEM 2024 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11000565/)
- Second meta-analysis: 3-point POCUS pooled sensitivity 89.15%, specificity 92.71%; recommends an attending with POCUS training be present — [Zaki et al., Ultrasound J 2024](https://theultrasoundjournal.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13089-024-00378-1.pdf)
- After a 10-minute training, residents found only 16 of 28 radiology-confirmed DVTs (~57% sensitivity) — [Zitek et al. 2016, WestJEM (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4786247/)

**Lung ultrasound**
- Expert-to-expert agreement is high (Fleiss kappa 0.87 among ICU experts; kappa 0.8–0.98 across studies), but drops for specific findings (pleural thickening kappa 0.33–0.48) — [Ultrasound J 2021 table](https://theultrasoundjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13089-021-00253-3/tables/3); [PMC8083133](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8083133)
- 25 novices vs 25 experts reading 40 B-line videos: near-perfect agreement among experts, only moderate among novices — [Ultrasound J 2021](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s13089-021-00234-6)
- Non-expert learners vs experts on B-lines: ICC 0.74 (learner–expert) vs 0.80 (expert–expert); medical students vs fellowship-trained attendings ICC 0.88 — [PMC7524048](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7524048); [PMC7005945](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7005945)
- A systematic review protocol (JBI, 2025) exists specifically because LUS "findings are largely dependent on operator technique and expertise"; results not yet published — [JBI protocol](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/reliability-of-lung-ultrasound-in-adult-patients-in-emergency-and-intensive-care-settings-a-systematic-review-protocol/)

**Thyroid**
- ACR TI-RADS overall category agreement is moderate-substantial (weighted kappa 0.72 in 272 nodules; 0.66 in 553 nodules), but when 15 *sonographers* assigned features, margin kappa was 0.18 and comet-tail 0.08 (slight) — [Indian J Radiol Imaging 2016](https://doaj.org/article/59ae237358a14a01b520545be2e9b96c); [Ain Shams 2024](https://asmj.journals.ekb.eg/article_365723.html); [Ultrasound Med Biol 2020 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32507343/)
- UK BTA U-classification: Fleiss kappa 0.30 then 0.19 across departmental operators (fair/slight) — [BMUS 2023 poster](https://bmus.org/poster-presentation-2023/evaluation-of-departmental-inter-rater-reliability-when-scoring-thyroid-nodules-acc-to-bta-u-classification-model-is-there-significant-disagreement)
- Piur Imaging's FDA-cleared freehand 3D thyroid sweep ("tUS inside", integrated with GE Logiq) claims automated volume/nodule assessment with "up to 80% less operator variability" — vendor claim, not independently verified — [AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ultrasound/news/15749880/piur-gets-fda-nod-for-new-ultrasound-device)

**MSK**
- 23 European experts scanning 24 rheumatic patients: overall agreement 83–91% by finding; kappa 0.61 (wrist/hand), 0.60 (knee), 0.50 (shoulder), 0.54 (ankle/foot); disagreement driven by dynamic exam and lesion definitions — [Naredo et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2006](https://ard.bmj.com/content/65/1/14)

### Inferences
- The exams where guidance software has demonstrated the biggest novice uplift (echo, OB, FAST) are exactly those where the core failure mode is *acquisition* (finding and holding a standardized window), not interpretation. Thyroid/MSK operator dependence is more about descriptor subjectivity, which 3D volume capture + offline review addresses differently (standardize what is captured, then let an expert or algorithm read it).
- A sweep/3D-map approach is best positioned for exams where completeness of coverage matters (thyroid, FAST free-fluid windows, OB sweeps, lung zones) and where the current "quality" label is itself unreliable (FAST QA kappa only slight) — an objective coverage map is a more defensible quality metric than a human "adequate/inadequate" grade.
- Novice sensitivity deficits (DVT 77% vs 93%; residents 57% after 10 minutes) are the clinical cost the product must show it closes.

### Gaps
- No single systematic review quantifies inter-operator variability across FAST, echo, OB, DVT, lung, thyroid, MSK together; figures above are from heterogeneous single studies with different statistics (kappa vs ICC vs limits of agreement) and are not directly comparable.
- Ma et al. 2005 novice-vs-expert FAST kappa values were not retrievable (abstract truncated).
- No experience-stratified sensitivity figures found for FAST comparable to the DVT review.

## KQ2. Sonographer workforce shortage data (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The US sonographer shortage is quantified and persistent: BLS projects 13% job growth 2024–2034 (~5,800 openings/yr), ASRT's 2025 survey shows a 12.4% sonography vacancy rate (down from 16.7% in 2023 but nearly double 2021's 6.9%), and exam volume grew faster than headcount over 2011–2021. The UK figure (24.2% vacancy, 2025 census) shows the problem is international.

### Cited Findings
- BLS Occupational Outlook: diagnostic medical sonographer employment projected to grow 13% from 2024 to 2034 ("much faster than average"); about 5,800 openings per year on average over the decade, many from replacement; median pay $89,340 (May 2024) — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Healthcare/Diagnostic-medical-sonographers.htm)
- O*NET/state trends page lists ~90,000 US sonographers in 2024 rising to ~101,700 by 2034 — [O*NET local trends](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localtrends/29-2032.00?st=IL&g=Go)
- ASRT 2025 Radiologic Sciences Staffing and Workplace Survey (published July 24, 2025; 475 department-manager responses out of 18,419 contacted): sonography vacancy rate 12.4%, down from 16.7% in 2023; CT highest at 19.4%; overall rates "at or near all-time highs" — [ASRT news release](https://www.asrt.org/news/article/2025/07/24/asrt-staffing-and-workplace-survey-shows-vacancy-rate-increases-near-record-highs-aligning-with-overall-health-care-profession-trends); [AuntMinnie summary](https://www.auntminnie.com/practice-management/article/15751626/asrt-survey-shows-vacancy-rates-still-high-among-imaging-departments)
- Trend: 6.9% (2021) → 16.7% (2023) → 12.4% (2025); SDMS president Aubrey Rybyinski discussed attrition causes on an AuntMinnie podcast (no SDMS-authored vacancy survey found) — [AuntMinnie MinnieCast ep. 14](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ultrasound/sonographer/video/15831868/ultrasound-minniecast-episode-14-weathering-the-sonographer-shortage)
- J Ultrasound Med 2024 ("Sound the Alarm"): US ultrasound exams rose 55.1% (2011–2021) while sonographer headcount rose 43.6%; open positions rose 36.3% (2012–2021); authors say the supply–demand gap had never been formally quantified before — [PubMed 38534218](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38534218/)
- UK Society of Radiographers 2025 Ultrasound Census (published March 2026; 153 provider responses): sonography vacancy rate 24.2%, regional peaks 38.2% — [SoR](https://www.sor.org/news/ultrasound/sonography-vacancy-rates-have-increased-dramatical); [PA/HelloRayo](https://www.hellorayo.co.uk/hits-radio/uk/news/ultrasound-staff-shortage-raises-fears-over-delays-vital-patient-scans)
- Rural access: trained sonographers and specialists concentrate in metropolitan settings; VA data show echo utilization drops significantly beyond 50 miles from a VA Medical Center; the rural sonographer shortage is described as "critical, long-standing, and worsening" — [Methodist DeBakey CV J review](https://journal.houstonmethodist.org/articles/1645)
- Canadian HTA: less than 28% of rural EDs have in-house ultrasound access — [CADTH/CJHT](https://canjhealthtechnol.ca/index.php/cjht/article/download/CM0021/2284?inline=1)
- Industry claim (unverified): rural patients "often wait 12 weeks for an echo or travel several hours" — [Jumpstart (vendor profile)](https://jumpstartinc.org/startup-news/optimizing-cardiac-care-with-cutting-edge-robotics-technology)

### Inferences
- The structural gap (volume growing ~25% faster than workforce over a decade) means demand for non-sonographer acquisition will persist even if vacancy rates ease cyclically.
- The strongest "paying pain" is where a sonographer is unavailable at all (rural EDs, home visits, LMIC clinics), not where one is merely busy.

### Gaps
- No SDMS-authored 2024–2026 vacancy survey located; SDMS appears to cite ASRT data.
- No US-specific rural echo wait-time survey found (only a Manitoba union statement of up to 1-year elective echo waits and an unverified 12-week industry figure).

## KQ3. POCUS in US medical schools/residencies; what students struggle with; existing training tools; is there a "learning tool" market for guided acquisition?

### Takeaway
As of the Dec 2024–Feb 2025 national survey, 66% of US MD schools (86 of 130 responding) have an approved POCUS curriculum, 87% of those with required elements, ~74% use handheld devices, but only 13 run four-year longitudinal programs and teaching drops off in clinical years with documented skill decay after ~8 weeks. Students' documented early barriers are probe handling and "getting the projections right." A real but modest simulator/education market exists (~$143M global ultrasound simulation market in 2023; SonoSim <$50/learner/month; ScanTrainer >500 installs; Butterfly 1:1 device programs at ~$2,000/device + $420/yr), and incumbents (GE/Caption, Butterfly ScanLab, Exo U, UltraSight) are already positioning guidance as a training accelerator.

### Cited Findings

**Curriculum penetration**
- 2025 national survey (data collected Dec 2024–Feb 2025; 155 MD schools surveyed, 130 responded, 84%): 66% have an approved POCUS curriculum (84 per abstract/86 per results); 75 of 86 (87%) have required elements; 13 schools require POCUS in all four years; required content most often in anatomy (41%), physical exam (37%), clerkships (28%); FAST (74%) and cardiac (76%) are the most-taught applications; 59 of 80 (~74%) use handheld machines; 61% assess students, mostly by test questions (89%), with hands-on/OSCE assessment at 62% of those assessing psychomotor skill; EM faculty teach at 94% of schools, only 6 schools use sonographers; up from 69 schools with curricula and 46 using handhelds in the 2020 survey — [Russell et al., J Ultrasound Med 2025 (PMC12605648)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12605648/)
- 2020 survey (published 2021): 57% of 122 responding MD schools had approved curricula; barriers were lack of trained faculty (63%), lack of curricular time (54%), lack of equipment (44%) — [IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/21809c4a-7dc7-47b5-ad2f-9f2db2b7819e)
- Osteopathic schools: 30 of 36 responding COMs (83.3%) incorporate focused ultrasound — [J Osteopath Med 2023](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/teaching-ultrasound-in-osteopathic-medical-schools/)
- Skill decay: POCUS skills decline significantly after as little as 8 weeks of non-use; teaching drops sharply from preclinical to clinical years — [Russell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12605648/)

**What learners struggle with**
- Echo learners identify two main early obstacles: "getting the projections right and handling the probe"; immediate feedback and risk-free practice helped — [BMC Med Educ 2019](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12909-019-1656-1)
- Ultrasound-naive first-year students (n=44): evaluator-rated difficulty obtaining views correlated with hands-on scores in all four categories — [BMC Med Educ 2021](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12909-021-02612-z)
- Credentialing thresholds that define "trained": ACEP-section model policy requires 25 scans per primary indication and 150 total for general EM ultrasound privileges, with QA review — [ACEP criteria PDF](https://www.acep.org/siteassets/uploads/uploaded-files/acep/membership/sections-of-membership/ultra/acep_criteria.pdf)

**Training-tool landscape and pricing**
- SonoSim: education pricing "<$50/mo/learner" (3-year term, 5-learner minimum, billed annually, plus onboarding) — [SonoSim groups page](https://sonosim.com/ultrasound-training-for-groups)
- CAE Vimedix: no public list price; sold by quote; CAE Blue Phantom reference-block ~CAD 812 for a basic vascular block (poster, non-peer-reviewed) — [Cureus poster](https://cureus.com/posters/2806-the-iterative-design-and-development-of-an-affordable-ultrasound-simulator)
- Global ultrasound simulation market ~USD 143.45M (2023) → USD 382.31M (2031), 13.4% CAGR (vendor-commissioned estimate) — via search summary of market-research press release; treat as directional (no primary link captured)
- Intelligent Ultrasound: sold its clinical AI business (ScanNav Assist → GE's SonoLyst) to GE HealthCare for ~$51M (announced July 18, 2024); the remaining simulation business (ScanTrainer, BodyWorks Eve, NeedleTrainer) was acquired by Surgical Science for £45.2M (13p/share; completed Feb 18, 2025); ScanTrainer reportedly >500 systems at >300 institutions in >30 countries (third-party profile) — [GE press release](https://www.gehealthcare.com/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/ge-healthcare-announces-agreement-to-acquire-clinical-artificial-intelligence-business-from-intelligent-ultrasound); [Surgical Science](https://surgicalscience.com/insights/news/surgical-science-acquires-intelligent-ultrasound/); [HealthySimulation profile](https://www.healthysimulation.com/vendors/intelligent-ultrasound/)
- Butterfly: iQ+ device ~$1,999 + $420/yr individual or $1,200/yr for up to 10 users (2019–2021 pricing); UK £1,699 + £360/yr (NICE 2023); schools giving every student a device include Temple (Class of 2025/2024, donor-funded), UC Irvine (Class of 2023, "just under $2,000" each), and KCU, which in Nov 2025 moved to a four-year 1:1 model with ScanLab (company press release) — [EP Monthly review](https://epmonthly.com/article/product-review-butterfly-iq-ultrasound/); [NICE MIB254](https://www.nice.org.uk/advice/mib254/chapter/summary); [Temple](https://templehealth.org/about/news/in-the-media/lewis-katz-school-of-medicine-is-the-first-medical-school-in-philadelphia-and-the-east-coast-to-provide-all-first-year-students-with-butterfly-iq-ultrasound-devices); [MobiHealthNews UCI](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/north-america/uc-irvine-medical-school-gifts-butterfly-handheld-ultrasounds-its-whole-class); [Butterfly KCU case study](https://www.butterflynetwork.com/press-releases/kcu-pocus-case-study)
- Butterfly ScanLab (AI education app, real-time feedback and anatomy labeling, included with membership) and "Educational View Guidance" (real-time feedback showing proportion of experts who would rate a cardiac/lung view measurable; labeled "Educational Use Only") — [Butterfly ScanLab release](https://www.butterflynetwork.com/press-releases/butterfly-education-scanlab); [Butterfly iQ Educational View Guidance manual](https://manual.butterflynetwork.com/Butterfly_iQ_Educational_View_Guidance-950-20019-00+rev+b-en.pdf)
- Butterfly Garden third-party AI marketplace: ThinkSono AI Training was first commercial partner (April 23, 2024) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240501128961/en)
- Exo U: in-app tutorials, anatomical guidance and feedback for novice POCUS learners — [MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/exo-launches-sweepai-ultrasound-applications/)
- GE HealthCare received >$44M from the Gates Foundation (Sept 2025) to build AI auto-assessment tools usable by staff "without specialised training", designed by the Caption team to run across devices including low-cost handhelds — [MedTech Spectrum](https://medtechspectrum.com/news/10/361/ge-healthcare-receives-44-m-grant-to-develop-ai-assisted-ultrasound-technology-for-lmics.html); [MD+DI](https://www.mddionline.com/artificial-intelligence/ge-healthcare-receives-boost-its-bid-design-ai-assisted-tools)

**Evidence that guidance works as a teaching tool**
- RCT of 44 novices: AI-enhanced group had better apical 4- and 5-chamber acquisition but took longer to finish the exam — [BGU publication record](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/ai-enhanced-guidance-demonstrated-improvement-in-novices-apical-4-2/)
- Stanford study: AI-integrated Kosmos group had faster scan times, higher image quality, better identification of reduced systolic function than non-AI group — [HospiMedica](https://mobile.hospimedica.com/medical-imaging/articles/294797517/ai-integrated-poc-ultrasound-devices-significantly-improve-competency-among-novice-users.amp.html)
- UltraSight secondary analysis (EHJ-Digital Health, May 2026): 9 novices (nurses + medical students), 159 patients, 3 centers, 8 hours of training; 97.7% of images met diagnostic threshold; all 9 reached diagnostic quality on first independent scan (company press release) — [AdvaMed/UltraSight](https://www.advamed.org/industry-updates/news/ultrasight-study-shows-novices-achieve-diagnostic-quality-cardiac-ultrasound-after-one-day-of-ai-guided-training/)
- Internal-medicine trainee RCT (n=43; EchoNous with AI vs Butterfly without) exists as a medRxiv preprint — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.12.23288497)

### Inferences
- The "learning tool" market is real but small and price-sensitive: schools pay tens of dollars per learner per month for software (SonoSim) and ~$2k per device; big-ticket simulators are capital purchases. A guidance product sold *to schools* will compete with features already bundled free into Butterfly/Exo memberships.
- The unmet educational need the survey data expose is assessment and retention (only 61% assess; hands-on assessment is minority practice; skills decay in 8 weeks). An objective, automatic "did the learner cover the region and capture standard views" score — which a 3D coverage map naturally produces — maps onto a gap incumbents' real-time view-quality meters do not fully fill.
- Because incumbents' guidance is device-locked (Caption on GE Venue/Vscan, UltraSight on Lumify/EchoNous, Exo on Iris), a device-agnostic guidance layer has a positioning opening but faces the regulatory "accessory to compatible systems" requirement (see KQ5).

### Gaps
- The 2025 survey did not measure barriers; latest barrier percentages are from 2020.
- No residency-level (GME) national count of guided-acquisition tool adoption found.
- No independent evaluation of Butterfly ScanLab or Exo U learning outcomes found; KCU data are company-published.

## KQ4. Trials of AI guidance for novices, including blind-sweep lines

### Takeaway
Two distinct paradigms are now backed by peer-reviewed evidence. (1) Real-time view guidance (Caption, UltraSight, EchoNous) lets nurses/novices obtain diagnostic-quality echo in >90% of patients after 1–8 hours of training, but still fails on hard windows (IVC 57%) and takes longer (median 30 min for 10 views). (2) Blind-sweep protocols (UNC/Zambia, Google, Marini's VSI) remove view-finding entirely: AI from standardized sweeps estimates gestational age with MAE 3.2–4.2 days, equal or better than expert biometry, including with one-day-trained novices on low-cost handhelds (JAMA 2024, n=400; JAMA Netw Open July 2026, n=385). The sweep paradigm is the closest published analog to a "complete scan + 3D map" product.

### Cited Findings

**Real-time guidance — cardiac**
- Narang et al., JAMA Cardiology, Feb 18, 2021 (Caption Guidance): prospective, 2 academic hospitals; 8 nurses with no echo experience, 1-hour didactic + 9 practice scans; 240 patients (30/nurse); primary endpoints met at 98.8% (LV size), 98.8% (LV function), 92.5% (RV size), 98.8% (pericardial effusion) vs 80% performance goal; secondary adequacy: RV function 91.1%, LA 94.5%, AV 91.5%, MV 96.2%, TV 83.0%, IVC 57.4% (vs 91.5% sonographer); median 10-view acquisition time 30 min (range 9–82). Author-stated limitations: small n, non-consecutive recruitment, no ICU/ED patients, nurses had medical backgrounds, no untrained-novice control, only 2 academic sites, no Doppler/interpretation, lay users untested. Funded by Caption Health; 6 authors employees, 6 consultants; independent statistician — [PMC8204203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8204203/)
- UltraSight pivotal (announced Nov 2022; U Chicago, Aurora St. Luke's, Sheba; PI Roberto Lang): 9 residents/nurses without echo experience scanned 240 patients for 10 standard views after short training; company reports diagnostic-quality images in 100% of patients; blinded review vs sonographers — [CIToday](https://citoday.com/news/ultrasights-ai-guidance-assessment-software-technology-studied-to-enable-novice-cardiac-sonographers); [NYU TOV](https://tov.med.nyu.edu/news/fda-grants-clearance-for-nyu-startup-ultrasights-cardiac-ultrasound-technology/)
- UltraSight secondary analysis, EHJ-Digital Health (DOI 10.1093/ehjdh/ztag065; press release May 26, 2026): 9 novices, 159 patients, 3 centers, 8 h training; 97.7% diagnostic quality; LV dysfunction ruled out in 99.4%, LVH in 98.7% — [AdvaMed](https://www.advamed.org/industry-updates/news/ultrasight-study-shows-novices-achieve-diagnostic-quality-cardiac-ultrasound-after-one-day-of-ai-guided-training/)
- Mayo Clinic + UltraSight, JAMA Cardiology (reported Aug 2026; presented ESC 2026): novices with 4 hours of training acquired images used for automated moderate-or-greater aortic stenosis detection, 93% sensitivity / 96% specificity (press coverage; paper not accessed) — [Mayo News Network](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/?p=417632); [HospiMedica](https://mobile.hospimedica.com/medical-imaging/articles/294812874/ai-guided-ultrasound--enables-nonexpert-screening-for-aortic-stenosis.amp.html); an ACC.26 abstract separately reported 99.1% accuracy for AS screening — [DAIC](https://www.dicardiology.com/content/acc26-presentations-discuss-how-ultrasight-ai-enables-non-experts-perform-diagnostic)
- UltraSight raised $24M Series B2 (Sept 2026) to scale across US health systems — [HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/ultrasight-raises-24-million-to-expand-ai-guided-cardiac-ultrasound)

### Inferences
- The sweep line proves the thesis that a product can shift skill from the operator to the protocol + software: one day of training, low-cost handheld, non-inferior to experts. But every published sweep result is OB (gravid abdomen is a large, homogeneous, easy-to-sweep target); extension to FAST, thyroid, lung, MSK has only pilot-level, non-AI (VSI) evidence (88–96% visualization) and no accuracy outcomes.
- Real-time guidance evidence is company-sponsored and single-paradigm (cardiac). The consistent weak point — IVC/subcostal windows and longer scan times — is where a sweep/coverage-map product could show differentiated value.
- A new entrant's pivotal design template is now established: ~8–9 novices × ~25–30 patients each (n≈240), expert blinded panel, prespecified per-view diagnostic-quality endpoints with an 80% performance goal (Caption), or an equivalence margin versus expert measurement (±2 days GA).

### Gaps
- Full text of the NEJM Evidence 2022 and JAMA 2024 papers not accessed; figures come from abstracts/preprint listing and trade press (consistent across sources).
- JAMA Netw Open 2026 CIs, operator experience, device, and noninferiority margin not accessible from the media page.
- No RCT of AI guidance in US primary care or home health with outcomes found; Northwestern CMA trial and Jefferson pilot have no published results.
- No peer-reviewed novice study of Exo SweepAI or Butterfly's commercial guidance found.

## KQ5. FDA regulatory classification and path for AI ultrasound guidance software

### Takeaway
Guidance software is a Class II device under 21 CFR 892.2100 ("image acquisition and/or optimization guided by artificial intelligence", product code QJU), created by the Caption Guidance De Novo (DEN190040, granted Feb 7, 2020). Multiple QJU 510(k)s now exist as predicates (Caption K200755, K201992, K243065; UltraSight K223347 and K251416), and the 892.2100 devices are regulated as software *accessories* to compatible general-purpose ultrasound systems. The PCCP final guidance (Dec 2024) is in active use by Caption; the broader AI lifecycle guidance was still draft as of late 2025.

### Cited Findings
- DEN190040: Caption Guidance, requester Bay Labs Inc. (later Caption Health), received Aug 27, 2019, **granted Feb 7, 2020**, product code QJU, regulation 21 CFR 892.2100, classification "image acquisition and/or optimization guided by artificial intelligence", Radiology panel; PCCP authorized: No (per database page; note the K243065 summary says the initial PCCP was "authorized as part of DEN190040") — [FDA De Novo database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/denovo.cfm?ID=DEN190040)
- Caption Guidance 510(k) K200755: QJU, received Mar 23, 2020, cleared Apr 16, 2020 — [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm?ID=K200755); K201992 (QJU, Caption Health) was the PCCP update — [deviceclearance.com](https://deviceclearance.com/510k/K201992/)
- K243065 "Cardiac Guidance" (Caption Health/GE), cleared **Jan 15, 2025**: predicate = Caption Guidance; Class II, QJU, 21 CFR 892.2100 "Radiological Acquisition And/Or Optimization Guidance System"; indications: "intended to assist medical professionals in the acquisition of cardiac ultrasound images… an accessory to compatible general purpose diagnostic ultrasound systems… 2D-TTE for adult patients" for 10 named views (PLAX, PSAX-AV/MV/PM, AP4/5/2/3, SubC4, SC-IVC); prescription use. - Clinical-study size precedent for QJU: Caption De Novo pivotal used 8 novices × 30 patients = 240 patients with four prespecified primary endpoints at an 80% performance goal and blinded 5-expert panel — [PMC8204203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8204203/); UltraSight pivotal used 9 novices, 240 patients, 3 sites, blinded reviewers vs sonographer scans — [CIToday](https://citoday.com/news/ultrasights-ai-guidance-assessment-software-technology-studied-to-enable-novice-cardiac-sonographers)

### Inferences
- A new entrant with a cardiac-view-guidance claim can use a traditional 510(k) with Caption/UltraSight as predicates and a ~240-patient novice study; a claim for *other anatomies* (FAST, thyroid, OB, lung) under QJU would be a new indication within the same regulation — FDA would likely expect a comparable per-indication novice performance study, since the 892.2100 classification name is anatomy-agnostic but every cleared predicate is cardiac.
- The "3D map / coverage reconstruction" element is a separate functional claim: freehand 3D reconstruction (Piur) has been cleared via 510(k) as an ultrasound accessory; combining it with guidance would likely mean one submission with two technological characteristics (QJU guidance + 3D reconstruction/measurement) and predicates from both lines.
- The regulatory architecture forces an "accessory to compatible systems" posture: compatibility with each host ultrasound system must be labeled and validated (Caption's PCCP explicitly covers "new compatible ultrasound systems"), which favors a PCCP from day one.
- If the product is marketed only as an educational trainer (no patient-management claim), Butterfly's "Educational Use Only" labeling suggests a non-device or enforcement-discretion posture is used in practice — but this limits the product to the small education market (KQ3).

## KQ6. Reimbursement: CPT/payment for AI-guided acquisition; POCUS billing; whether guidance changes economics; what customers pay

### Takeaway
There is no professional-fee CPT code for AI *guidance*; POCUS with AI bills exactly as POCUS without AI (limited-study codes such as 76705, 76815, 93308, 76775, 76604, usually with -26), and only a physician's independent interpretation is payable. Facility-side novel-technology mechanisms have been used (Caption Guidance NTAP effective Oct 1, 2021, max reported add-on $1,868.10 per case, facility-paid; EchoGo HF got NTAP + a Category III code with ~$300 outpatient rate by late 2025). Software pricing is unpublished: Caption was bought for ~$150M (Feb 2023); Caption Care sells home echo as negotiated value-based bundles to MA plans.

### Cited Findings
- ACEP coding guidance: common limited-POCUS codes in the ED are 76705 (abdomen limited), 76815 (OB limited), 93308 (limited echo), 76775 (retroperitoneal limited), 76604 (chest); use -26 when the hospital owns the machine; use -52 (+ -26) when no limited code exists — [ACEP Now Coding Wizard](https://www.acepnow.com/article/coding-wizard-how-to-get-paid-for-point-of-care-ultrasound/)
- ACEP, Feb 23, 2026 ("AI in POCUS: billing and reimbursement overview"): POCUS using AI is billed the same as without AI provided the physician independently interprets; AI output is decision support; no pathway to bill an AI-only read regardless of FDA clearance; NTAP (inpatient, on top of DRG), TPT (hospital outpatient/ASC), NT-APC (outpatient clinics) are the CMS mechanisms for new technology; **Caption Guidance received NTAP in 2021 — payment goes to the facility and does not change the professional fee**; EchoGo Heart Failure (2023) obtained NTAP plus outpatient Category III CPT payment, with a nationally established outpatient rate of ~$300 by late 2025; Health Tech Investment Act (introduced Apr 2025) would create a predictable 5-year Medicare payment pathway for algorithm-based tools — not law — [ACEP EM Ultrasound newsroom](https://www.acep.org/emultrasound/newsroom/february-2026/ai-in-pocus-an-overview-of-the-current-billing-and-reimbursement)

A medical-student evaluator should treat "who pays" as: hospitals buying hardware bundles (GE/Butterfly/Exo), payers/VBC entities buying a service, and schools buying education seats — not fee-for-service reimbursement of the software itself.
- The EchoGo HF precedent (~$300 outpatient rate via Category III + NTAP) shows a path exists for AI that delivers a *diagnostic output*, not for acquisition guidance alone; a 3D-map product that produced a reportable quantitative output (e.g., thyroid volume, free-fluid volume, GA) would be closer to that path.

- Specific 2026 wRVU values for 76705/93308/76815 not captured.
- Caption Care's current (2025–2026) operating status could not be confirmed.

### Inferences
- The incumbents own the two cheapest distribution channels (bundled into a handheld; bundled into a cart). A new standalone product most plausibly enters via (a) a device-agnostic educational/assessment layer sold to schools and residencies, or (b) a sweep/3D-coverage workflow for a non-cardiac indication (thyroid, FAST, OB) where no QJU predicate exists and grant-funded LMIC programs are actively buying novice-operable OB tools.
- For a medical-student evaluator: the "real, paying problem" is best documented for cardiac in US hospitals/VBC and for OB in LMIC programs; it is *asserted but undocumented* for rural clinics and US home health.
